# Final flight screen detail

The fixed final-flight setup was missing the projected-star diagram. Its camera also views the reverse side of the cloth, so adding light to the projector-facing side would not make the effect visible there. The final fixed illustration now uses its empty lower-left panel for a labeled **Screen detail**, looking at the receiving surface from the same side as the projector. The large whale and whole setup retain their previous pixels and framing.

`newflight_screen.py` loads only the existing `projected_light` function from the historical reward renderer's syntax tree. It does not execute that renderer's CLI or change historical sources. The helper uses the actual punched mask, current spindle phase, mounted projector transform and finite cloth bounds. Its two ten-vertex polygons are the same ideal chief-ray footprints used by the final garden reward, displaced 1 mm toward the projector solely to put the diagram on the cloth surface. The inset uses the reward's evening lighting, which makes the projected light readable without clipping the cloth to white. The camera preserves the complete projected footprint at its true relative scale.

The final whale is 1 m above the cradle. Its star-disc phase is approximately 0.189473689 radians. Star 44 occupies world x=0.274965–0.294038 m, z=1.487697–1.506190 m; star 79 occupies x=0.186823–0.205884 m, z=1.448028–1.466567 m. Both lie inside the unchanged cloth's x=−0.205–0.695 m, z=1.275–1.725 m bounds. The renderer checks all ten vertices against the helper's predicted footprint, all stars against the cloth and camera bounds, and exact equality of every existing geometry object's world transform before and after the new view.
