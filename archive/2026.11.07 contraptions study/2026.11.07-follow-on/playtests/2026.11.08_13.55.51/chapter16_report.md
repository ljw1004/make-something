## Comparison of choices across players

### Choosing where the first cup belongs

All three faced the same cup-fitting goal, objects and illustrated descriptions. Player 1 attempted **Cup (#1) + Claw at local step 1**, using the projecting pins to infer a seat on the board; they explicitly considered inspecting the cup but chose a direct test. Players 2 and 3 instead combined **Cup (#1) + Output syringe at their respective local step 1**: Player 2 named the likely cylinder, while Player 3 cited the cup’s round opening and apparent size match. Neither listed an inspection alternative.

This is reasoned divergence in attachment hypotheses, not merely a different execution order. It supports the possibility of interpretation at this goal and the grader’s borderline credit for Player 1’s concrete test. It does not establish that the successful choices required substantive discrimination: the other graders explicitly found their reasoning insufficient to distinguish plausible syringe recipients. Those classifications remain unchanged. Automatic redirection gave Player 1 the same supported syringe as the others, so equal route lengths conceal the initial misunderstanding.

### Choosing where the plunger clip belongs

After fitting both cups, all three attempted **Plunger clip + Syringe at their respective local step 3**, expecting attachment to the syringe. Player 1 treated the goal as indicating that assembly; Player 2 explicitly reasoned that the syringe was the assembly currently being built; Player 3 called it the likely attachment. All received automatic attachment to the claw instead. Player 1 already had experience of a redirected cup attempt, whereas Players 2 and 3 had reached this point through successful direct combinations.

The shared error supports an instruction-clarity concern: the goal did not communicate the intended recipient to any player. It does not by itself prove either meaningful experimentation or arbitrary guessing. The independent judgments differ at a narrow boundary: Player 2’s context-based test receives meaningful credit, while Players 1 and 3’s assumptions do not. Player 2 explicitly reports surprise; the others emphasize clarification. These differences offer some support for the distinction, but the closely similar choices and corrections do not decisively resolve the grading threshold. All original classifications and counts are retained.
