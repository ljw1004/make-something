# Contraptions: post-mortem

**Decision, 9 November 2026: abandon the story.** We did not find anything
worthwhile. Abandoning the project was the right call.
This is the outcome, not a pause while another narrative or invention is proposed.

## What we tried

The premise borrowed the appeal of physical construction kits: recognizable parts,
clear instructions, visible assembly and a machine that works when finished. The
user enjoyed some early creations, especially the spinning wheel. Those moments
were real, but they did not sustain interest in assembling a succession of assigned
contraptions. The work became samey and pointless.

We made substantial improvements to the presentation and physical account: larger
parts, bounded scales, movable workpieces, separate repeated fittings, explicit
folds and flips, goal pictures, shorter labels, less prose, consistent geometry and
working animations. These addressed genuine problems. They did not answer why the
player should care about doing the work.

Later attempts supplied future technology, mysterious senders, a rescue and a
sinister collector. They tried to give the constructions a larger reason to exist.
The story felt forced, and increasingly elaborate motives did not earn investment
in the underlying objects. A final reset tested ordinary useful inventions: a crumb
sweeper and a snack cable carrier, with shorter assembly and more explicit causal
feedback. That investigation also failed to establish a worthwhile direction.

## Where the process went wrong

We repeatedly treated evidence that a construction made sense as evidence that it
was worth doing. A gear belongs on an axle; a wheel supports a trolley; a carrier
can deliver a biscuit. None of those facts establishes the value of enacting each
fitting in this game. A useful finished object and a worthwhile sequence of digital
combinations are different claims.

A physical kit also offers experiences this format did not reproduce: handling
materials, solving fit problems, making something one owns, then using and altering
it. Clear pictures and an animation do not automatically transfer that value into
prescribed drags. This is a design limitation to examine, not proof that digital
construction games cannot work.

The project accumulated production work and review rounds before demonstrating a
hook strong enough to justify its scale. After each failure, another narrative,
contraption or refinement became the next hypothesis. The existing investment
should not have made continuation the default. We should have used the worthwhile
criterion to stop sooner.

## What the evaluations do and do not say

The final study contains twenty actual AI chapter sessions, including three
faulty-caption wording pilots excluded from assembly comparisons and a Creation
control. Some participants valued a mechanical relationship or the final delivery.
Under revised questions, three final Carrier players called their passage worth
the effort while declining another similar chapter. Changing the question also
changed the judgment on an unchanged passage.

Those results remain in the archive. They are limited local responses, sensitive
to wording and supplied context, not evidence against the user's rejection. Text
players did not experience the artwork or animation, and chapter checkpoints did
not give them the experience of building the preceding chapters. Neither favorable
phrases nor technical completion should have been converted into optimism about
the whole project.

## What to carry forward

The [authoring lessons](../../docs/story-authoring.md#cautionary-tale-contraptions)
are to test the value of the work the player actually performs, distinguish that
from the usefulness of the result, evaluate casual instruction-following honestly,
and accept abandonment when the hook does not emerge. Ordinary predictable actions
can be worthwhile; the remedy is not to hide instructions or invent artificial
puzzles. Better usability and sound engineering remain valuable, but they cannot
supply missing subject matter.

The story, prototypes, artwork and reviews are retained for learning. There is no
remaining drawing-machine experiment, native-3D phase or proposed narrative for
this project. Useful shared tooling survives independently; Contraptions is closed.
