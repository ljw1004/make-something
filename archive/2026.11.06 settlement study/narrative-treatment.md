# Settlement: narrative treatment

Current 28-chapter outline, 9 November 2026. The [live design brief](../../stories/settlement/design-brief.md) owns accepted intent; [outcomes](outcomes.md) distinguish tested versions.

## Food, shelter and useful craft

1–6 retain the established making sequence: stone and brick, first supper, house, brook crossing, mill and bread. Added subgoals make growing plants, the sleeping mat and the cart wheel visible. Plant has an experimental larger icon. The house opening uses the user's short river-side motivation.

7–8 make pottery, then preserves. A wooden jar lid has its own subgoal. 9 brings charcoal into ironworking as furnace fuel, alongside kiln, cloth bellows, tools and a chest. There is no separate charcoal chapter. 10–11 shelter and support the garden, then irrigate it, retaining staged chisel and saw guidance.

12 combines protected drinking water, wash-water drainage and soap. 13 makes and uses a movable stove with an iron cooking pot. The original wall hearth remains in the background as scenery; there is no Kitchen hearth hotspot or ceramic-to-metal pot conversion.

## Harbour and winter stores

14 builds a dock and sailboat for fishing and trade. 15 makes the oil lamp, lantern and lighthouse. The tower now sits higher on the headland. 16 fishes beyond the shallows and smokes the catch. 17 fills an empty grain bin, dries fruit and pickles fruit for winter.

## Festival and studio

18–20 prepare a harvest festival with cloth decorations, music and dancing, and puppet theatre for children. The festival is now an explicitly accepted motive for this short section, not for the settlement's infrastructure.

21 moves indoors after the festival to painting and mosaic. The arcade/gallery chain is removed. 22 groups printing and clockwork as mechanical projects in the studio. The portable printing press still accepts ink or paper first.

## Investigation

23 studies plants, making a pressed specimen and a separate drawing. 24 looks closer through a microscope, directly placing pond water on glass and producing one illustrated study. 25 observes weather at the beach, making separate rain and wind records. The clock supports equal measurement intervals. 26 studies the stars after dark; only the mounted telescope produces the star chart.

## The settlement's record

27 returns to the studio by oil light, viewed from another angle, to compile the encyclopedia. Printed artwork, pressed specimen, botanical drawing, pond study, rain record, wind record and star chart fit individually in any order. No records were bundled earlier. Binding boards are prepared only at the binding table and remain fixed there as the records are added; the completed volume stays on the table. The opening and result are deliberately short: compile what we have learned; here it is in the book.

28 leaves the finished record for descendants in a modest settlement room. There is no large library or departure task.

## Artwork and evidence

Artwork generation is authorized. The background audit keeps the opening's warm growing season rather than claiming literal spring despite ripe fruit. Autumn festival and studio scenes lead to dusk at the beach, astronomy at night, and lamplit binding. The closing room is at dawn. Existing geographic and construction continuity is retained where it works.

Production assets replace the mismatched backgrounds and labelled placeholders. Single-object icons preserve vessel materials and omit explanatory ingredients placed beside an output. Exact prompts, selected masters and export details are retained under [artwork](artwork/); runtime exports live beside the manuscript. Focused fast playtests use fresh Terra players and SOL graders. Their results remain attributed model evidence, separate from browser mechanics and human enjoyment.
