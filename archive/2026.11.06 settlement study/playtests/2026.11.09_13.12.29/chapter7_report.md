At the same time, those actions suggest broader developments in human societies: making building materials and shelter, preparing food, moving supplies with a wheeled cart, using water power to grind grain, and firing glazed pottery.

The broader connection is suggestive rather than a reliable history. The game compresses complex processes into combinations and presents them in a single sequence, without showing how practices varied among societies or developed over time. Some recipes are especially abstract: clay plus a formed bowl produces a jar without a clear physical explanation, and glazing the bowl also fires it in one step. Those details work better as game actions than evidence of how such developments occurred.
