# Builder: making worth doing

## Current development target

This is the single brief for Builder and its study work. Live v48 remains the
preceding thirteen-toy game. The current [worthwhile study](../../projects/builder-study/worthwhile-study.md)
starts over with small ordinary inventions and actual player iterations. Earlier
alien, collector, world-rescue and future-technology treatments are set aside.
No existing construction has a reserved place. Do not build another overarching
story to justify a weak collection of objects.

## Intent and research question

For a casual player around ten who mechanically follows instructions and drags
whatever fits, what makes doing these combinations worthwhile? Clear instructions,
physical plausibility, an attractive result and completion alone do not answer it.
The user enjoyed the spinning wheel and some early constructions, but repeated
assigned assembly became samey and pointless. Preserve that distinction.

Start with one project of two to five chapters and at least one genuine physical
subassembly. Test it, revise it from concrete player evidence, then try a different
contraption and revise again. Investigate useful capabilities, recognizable causal
understanding, anticipated material transformation, expressive output, satisfying
motion and modest situational purpose. None is presumed effective. A whole object
should do something the player has reason to care about beyond finishing a recipe.

Use ordinary inventions with minimal story. A room, a household request, a delivery
across a gap or another immediate circumstance can supply a reason without a plot
lecture. Situations and wider episodes may grow from useful work later. Do not
introduce aliens, distant societies or pink future technology in this study.
Commercial kit restrictions are not the limit of invention: modest idealizations
such as a candle-heated boiler or simple model aircraft are allowed. Keep the
assumption explicit in author notes and preserve recognizable cause and effect.
Most pages are quiet instructions. Omit routine introductions, praise and result
popups. A brief request or a working scene can communicate purpose; do not explain
an overarching narrative or tell the player how to feel.

Judge the digital experience actually offered. Fixed results and completion loops
do not provide the adjustable play, control or authorship of a physical toy. Do not
claim those pleasures on behalf of the player. Conversely, an obvious action may
still be satisfying or worthwhile; do not invent puzzles merely to raise decision
scores. Remove or combine assembly steps whose cost is not earned by experience.

## Evaluation approach

Use real shared-engine chapter sessions with fresh participants. In addition to
the normal questions and desire to continue, ask during steps and at chapter end
what they got from doing the work, what felt pointless or arbitrary, and whether
the played passage was worth doing and how the resulting project matters to them.
Keep that answer separate from wanting another chapter. A chapter participant has
not experienced the effort of earlier checkpoint history; do not ask or report as
though they built the whole project. Explicitly supply a casual instruction-following
mindset. Compare direct and ordinary-language ways of asking; choose for specificity
and honesty, not higher scores. Keep missing and indifferent responses visible.

Retain the existing Worthwhile and Fun definitions, and separately record grounded
step-value reports, arbitrary-step reports and project-level judgments. Do not
silently redefine the standard score. A functional explanation, a reported reason
to continue and actual enjoyment are separate findings. Low-count AI trials are
hypothesis generators, not children or population estimates. Word-only proposed
pictures and use scenes must be labeled as such until actual artwork is inspected.

The study owns candidate comparisons, exact additional guidance, experimental
counts, revisions and outcome tables. Its selected playtest arrays and chapter
reports follow the standing evaluation policy. This brief owns the adopted scope
and continuing construction rules. No full-length story or 200-action target is
required before a single project proves worth further development.

## Construction and instructions

Every workpiece is a movable item, including intermediate and finished stages.
Hotspots are actual processes, shown as outlined drawings. Adding a part visibly
incorporates that part; changing the existing workpiece's shape or orientation is
an explicit process such as Fold, Roll, Cut, Punch or Flip. Installing a flexible
cord or cable can show its real fitted route, but cannot silently alter unrelated
existing parts. Never hide an automatic flip or lid closure inside an attachment.

Each draggable picture depicts one connected physical part or retained mechanism.
Repeated components are separate supplies and additions. Identical pieces may have
the same short name and different internal IDs; physically interchangeable copies
must not require an invisible matching puzzle. Factory modules are allowed when
ordinary and clearly modelled: a gearmotor, connected bearing fitting, or battery
holder with attached leads. A label must not turn a scattered pile into one part.
Loose lids, pairs of wings, mirror strips, coloured chips, wheels and guards must
be handled honestly. A prepared pole/horse unit becomes one part only after the
player has joined it.

Use coherent subassembly chapters, usually with 4–8 starting pieces. A bounded
larger set is acceptable when literal construction needs it and the real browser
remains usable; the racer chassis has ten movable pieces plus Flip. Short everyday
labels are visual anchors, not miniature descriptions. Goals repeat those nouns
and show one concrete next step through `next:` chains. Preserve sensible alternate
assembly orders, rather than treating the instruction's suggested order as a new
physical constraint. Goal dependencies must not preserve obsolete intermediates
and duplicate unique components.

Every operation needs a visible contribution. For every addition, the attachment
site must face the viewer and occupy enough screen space for the fitted part to be
recognized. Merely including it in the model is insufficient. A hidden back-face
addition requires a preceding Flip; a small joint needs an appropriate working view
or a larger assembly scale. Inspect the actual before/after pictures at phone size.
The loose part should be presented in its fitting orientation; use an explicit
process when the existing assembly must turn. Do not silently mirror or rotate a
workpiece between an additive pair. Assemble real repeated parts when
each addition is clear, but do not pad the story with individual identical screws.
Completion is the final useful fitting. Remove weak tests, staged failures, scenery
placement and compulsory operation sequences that only rename an existing object.
The purpose is satisfying guided making, not guessing which hidden recipe the
writer intended.

Physical tools remain tools; incorporated material is consumed. Current story
semantics come from the shared specification. Treat physical scale and operation
as part of the invention. The current study permits ordinary mechanisms outside commercial children’s kits and modest stated
engineering idealizations; it is not restricted to the former fictional tile system.

## Presentation and handling

Instruction pages are white. Use simple geometry and flat material colours:
honey wood, **lighter** forest green and brick red, cream and charcoal hardware.
Battery cases share a distinct blue material; the current ordinary-invention study
does not use pink advanced modules. Leads, cells and controls retain their ordinary
material colours.
Colour should suggest material or function rather than reproduce a kit brand's
combined palette. The central production palette lives in the study's
`materials.py`. No woodgrain, cloth maps or decorative texture noise. Preserve real
openings and real material transparency; do not make solid wood translucent to
help the interface. Keep source models suitable for a later compact 3D renderer.

Each chapter has a short visible title and a titled picture of its actual construction target. The reference is
visibly distinct from loose parts: a very pale framed panel high in the right-hand
third, with only a short object title alongside the picture. Its enlarged view remains available. The [UX guide](../../docs/ux.md#construction-reference-pictures)
owns dimensions and placement; debug controls do not constrain its gameplay layout.
Up to two process drawings occupy the other thirds. A single process is centered on the page. When Flip and Fold share a page, **Flip is on
the left and Fold in the middle**, giving large paper a central folding target.
Enlarged receiving areas around process art make drops forgiving without enlarging
the drawings or overlapping the reference.

Size is essential. Loose parts, intermediate work and finished toys have different
scale families, with caps on very large and very small pieces. Broad opaque
workpieces are normally around half the page width; compact open assemblies may
be larger. Finished toys stay generously sized. A carried subassembly becomes
secondary until fitted; unrelated completed toys stay behind. Show slender parts
diagonally where helpful and retain at least the ordinary 44px touch area.

Use a stable camera and physical scale within an additive group. Crop each current
picture to its actual visible geometry, with only a small antialiasing margin. Do
not reserve transparent space or a selection outline for parts added in future
steps. Stable physical scale does not require identical image-canvas dimensions. Explicit flips, a new
working face, or the completed-object view may change perspective to expose the
relevant structure. Magnification may change between roles; fitted physical
proportions never do. Actual tops and undersides must be distinguishable—for
example, flat fitting marks can identify the mast socket and bearing seats without
texture maps. Test the whole sequence at short-phone sizes, with readable current
goals and exposed pickup points. Some recoverable tabletop overlap is acceptable;
it must not make the next step impossible or require knowing an invisible target.

Prefer substantial central bodies: trays, palms, decks, enclosed optical toys and
compact mechanisms. Reject or rethink inherently spindly constructions before
rendering them. Do not invent irrelevant backing plates merely to make a bad shape
fit. Held items follow the finger beyond the page and fit back on ordinary release,
according to the shared dragging contract.

## Physical models and evaluation

Canonical dimensioned Blender assemblies are the source of truth for loose parts,
partial constructions, completed toys and later installations. Use metres and a
consistent coordinate convention. Retain part ownership and useful motion controls.
A shared model must preserve actual holes, pivots, shaft axes, clearances, cable
lengths, insertion routes and component identity. An attractive camera cannot
conceal a missing drive or an impossible fitting.

Prototype mechanics before final rendering. Check complete motion ranges for cams,
followers, links, flippers, pulleys, syringes, jaws and rotating assemblies. Review
actual evaluated geometry and several views with fresh participants. Distinguish
nominal geometric checks and prescribed flex envelopes from material simulation,
retention forces, manufactured reliability or child-safe operating loads. Fix rigid
intersections and blocked assembly paths; describe evidence limits candidly.

Whole completed objects have looping working animations, normally 5–10 seconds
at about 15 frames per second. Intermediate subassemblies remain still. The first
frame preserves the finished still's viewpoint and fitted geometry. Camera movement
is optional and supports understanding; the existing pinwheel both spins and receives a
modest camera orbit. Drive parts, followers, links and wheels must move consistently
with their actual mechanism. Use the canonical models, simple materials and compact
exports. A lower raster resolution must preserve its authored display size. Keep
still reference pictures and a reduced-motion alternative. The finished
machine's visible behavior must earn its construction; working motion can be the
primary reward. Do not add an arbitrary operate/test recipe to reach it. The final narrative journey may use a longer one-shot sequence with recognition holds. A Three.js runtime remains a separate phase.

Follow the standing authoring and evaluation guides. Use informed necessity and
mechanical reviews, fresh visual/continuity reviews, actual source-unexposed
`--fast` and `--detailed` playtests, and real browser pointer/touch-emulation checks.
Text tests assess instructions and combinations, not the visual satisfaction the
user enjoyed in the opening. Preserve that human evidence without treating it as
proof for the new later chapters. Test major changed families, sensible alternative
orders, complete routes, reload and chapter transitions. Revise weak passages
before expensive artwork; do not add narrative or guessing merely to inflate a
text-only deliberation score.

The live manuscript and accepted artwork belong in `stories/builder`; ongoing
source models, drafts, actual playtests and concise outcomes belong in
`projects/builder-study`. This is their single active design brief. Remove temporary
renders, captures and detailed verification packets after recording outcomes.
