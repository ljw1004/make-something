# Settlement design brief

This records the story’s narrative, visual and geographic intent for future edits. Shared interaction behaviour belongs in [UX](../../docs/ux.md), and reusable illustration methods in the [icon guide](../../docs/icon-guide.md).

Settlement takes its name from the settler who begins a civilization. Its worthwhile hook is making the means of a shared life from raw materials: food, shelter, craft, care, art and investigation. A loaf baked in this settlement can stand for people learning to bake; a local scene should make a lasting human capability tangible. Let particular people and familiar places carry that wider significance without turning the whole settlement into preparations for one visit or event. The harvest festival now deliberately motivates the short weaving, music and puppetry sequence; it does not explain sanitation, trade or scientific work.

Write in concrete, direct language about materials, work, mechanisms and observable results. Let the reader infer the broader significance. Avoid aphorisms, grand claims, mysterious hints, personified abstractions and sentences explaining what an invention means for civilization. Chapter titles should name the work, objects or setting plainly; avoid riddles, metaphors and hints at a larger meaning. Chapter openings should establish a plain, concrete situation, need or desire, then explain why the work matters. They are motivating prose, not a list of goals rewritten as sentences and not a recipe sequence. A higher-level intention is useful when it explains what the work enables: crossing a blocked path, carrying supplies, keeping food through winter, sharing music or investigating something unfamiliar. Put staged material guidance in visible subgoals and mechanisms in discovery readings. Popups should describe the result or explain a useful physical relationship. Omit a closing italic object name when the picture or prose already identifies the result. Retain one only when it helps name an unfamiliar tool or material, as with Bellows and Lye; the repeated title idiom is mainly useful for abstract advances such as Theology. This does not remove the inline scientific-field names in chapter introductions. Avoid incidental scheduling such as "on a later morning" and household visits as default motives. Keep warmth in specific details and ordinary human actions, as in the child looking through the microscope. Do not add a thematic moral after them.

Use the encyclopedia for the factual craft explanations and sources behind the combinations. Keep established discovery popups unchanged when expanding these entries; add reference-only readings for objects that need no interruption. Entries should explain the object or process directly, with relevant sources and useful limitations, rather than reproduce author notes about recipe compression or inventory rules. Keep artwork continuity, geometry, implementation choices and remaining abstraction constraints in author comments. Link sources directly from the entry instead of hiding them in a private reference list.

Do not automatically copy every popup into the encyclopedia. Construction-status messages and reminders about missing ingredients can be popup-only; the fuller reference belongs with the underlying tool, material or process. Short readings that explain a real mechanism can remain concise, as with the lantern's enclosure and ventilation. Where a substantial subject has only a generic announcement, add a concrete explanation or revealing example rather than padding it with claims of importance. The Encyclopedia entry should explain arranging and finding knowledge as well as the physical binding of a book.

Protect the established early making and home-bank geography. Preserve the opening craft sequence and material relationships through **Baking bread**, adding the requested plant/wood growth, sleeping-mat and wheel subgoals where they clarify the work. Plant uses its original normal-sized icon; the larger-icon experiment was rejected. Its discovery popup hints at the kinds of plants and their useful combinations through seed heads, watering, woody trunks and crushed fibres; avoid generic statements of usefulness or unrelated plant biology. Begin house-building with wood growth, automatically passing that subgoal when Wood was already discovered. The motivation audit applies to chapter prose throughout, including the opening; retain passages that already establish a useful situation and purpose. In the rest of the early game, preserve the material relationships while allowing the accepted pottery-before-preserving order, practical infrastructure motives and coherent regrouping. Shape, glaze and fire bowls and jars at the potter's kiln before using the vessels to preserve food. Fishing and trade motivate the harbour and lighthouse; visiting friends or festival guests do not explain that infrastructure. The later food chapters prepare and store food for the settlement, without returning to provisioning boats. Gardening, irrigation and food storage belong in the basic-provision stretch. Let earlier constructions find concrete uses in later chapters, rather than linking unrelated tasks with a new errand or a date.

Follow the rough accumulation of capabilities: meet basic food and shelter needs, develop craft, agriculture and storage, provide drinking water and hygiene, equip transport and trade, then develop public arts, systematic investigation and a reference work. Chronology is approximate: technologies can move earlier and related crafts can be grouped when that makes the progression clearer. Trellises and irrigation fit with early food production; they need not wait for a late stage. Make the oil lamp as the first part of the lighthouse chapter, and let that lamp actually supply the tower’s light. Do not give it a separate harbour-watch errand. Do not keep a lighting task solely to supply a later departure vignette.

Keep related work together across several chapters. Transitions should follow a continuing purpose or a useful earlier result. Keep the two food-preparation chapters adjacent: smoke fish, then fill an empty grain bin, dry fruit and pickle fruit for winter. The completed harvest leads into a harvest festival: decorate with cloth, make instruments for music and dancing, then puppet theatre for children. This accepted seasonal occasion replaces the previous restriction on festival motivation for these chapters only. After the festival, move indoors to painting and mosaic in an art studio. A second studio chapter groups the printing press and clock as mechanical work. Do not retain the arcade/gallery construction merely to house the pictures. These broad stages are approximate, not a strict archaeological chronology or a ranking of cultures.

Sanitation and hygiene precede the public arts and scientific investigations. Drinking-water preparation, drainage and soap belong in one shared-facilities chapter. Enact shared provision: protected drinking water, used washing water carried away separately, and soap for handwashing. A basin for visiting guests or a renamed household object is insufficient. Show what the facilities do through their construction and use. Distinguish water's visible clarity from microbial safety, preparation from clean storage, and wash-water drainage from sewage treatment or a complete sanitation system. No microscopic pond observation proves a water supply safe or establishes germ theory. Retain the story's craft abstractions and appropriate factual limits.

Investigations proceed from botany to microscopy, weather and astronomy. Give each a short, plain introduction to the interest in that field; naming *Botany*, *Microscopes*, *Meteorology* and *Astronomy* is welcome. Botany makes a pressed specimen and a separate drawing. Microscopy makes one illustrated Pond life study, without a second Pond drawings operation. Glass and pond water directly prepare the slide; omit the arbitrary glass-and-planks step. Weather makes separate rain and wind records, with the clock used for equal observation intervals. Only the mounted telescope can produce the star chart. Do not combine botanical or weather papers early.

The encyclopedia is the culmination, bound by lamplight back in the studio, seen from another angle. Its seven contributions are Printed pages (art), Pressed plant specimen, Botanical drawing, Pond life study, Rain record, Wind record and Star chart. All fit directly in any order. The boards and finished encyclopedia stay fixed on the binding table while records are brought to them. Prepare the binding boards only at the Binding table so the book cannot be assembled during earlier investigations. Reserve binding for this chapter; avoid repeated book-filling during the rest of the game. Its opening is simply the intention to compile what we have learned. The result needs no instructions on how to read a book. The finished encyclopedia is a printed volume: reproduce the gathered observations as ink illustrations on its pages, without tape, pasted specimens or scrapbook styling. Keep its covers, binding and printed spread continuous with the book in the final room. The Binding table receiving rectangle must exactly match the Binding boards at their intended tabletop location, so its highlight does not change size or position when the boards appear. Keep it away from the lamp and table legs. Close with the record left for descendants in a modest settlement room, not a full library and not a sailing task.

Printing stays movable and uses carved relief blocks, not movable type. Paper and ink can be added in either order: Uninked press or Inked press, then Printed pages. These are alternative states of one machine, without a hotspot-placement step. The printed artwork contributes to the encyclopedia without a separate festival-documentation task.

Guide unfamiliar workshop steps with staged subgoals: growing plants, making a sleeping mat and wheel, making a wooden jar lid, kiln and cloth bellows before the forge and pickaxe, nails at the forge before the tool chest, and a chisel and saw before irrigation construction. Keep the kiln/bellows/forge goals short: Build a kiln, Make cloth bellows, Get it hotter. Charcoal belongs in ironworking as fuel rather than a thin standalone chapter before sanitation. Keep the cloth-bellows abstraction without adding animals or leather. Name the workshop tool **Chisel** and explain cutting saw teeth; distinguish the wide **Water pipe** from the slender **Wooden tube** by purpose and form. Explain cutting, boring and finishing as compressed workshop operations rather than claiming a saw drills a pipe.

Name incomplete objects for what they lack: **Unplumbed washstand → Washstand**. The normal finished object should not acquire an adjective merely because its missing part has been fitted. Preserve material and vessel continuity in the art: filling a glazed ceramic jar with pickles does not turn it into glass. Existing matching art can be reused. Avoid pictures of an output beside its ingredients: filtered water is one wooden cup, the drinking-water bowl has no extra cup, the leaf study has no lens beside it, and pond water uses the same fired glazed bowl, visibly filled near the rim with a reflective water surface distinct from the empty blue glaze. The grain bin is visibly empty before grain fills it. Cooking uses a movable Stove with a fitted iron pot, without a Kitchen hearth hotspot; the pot does not derive from a glazed ceramic bowl. The whole pot sits above the stove top; its dark circle is a fitting target, not a hole that swallows the pot. Keep the bare stove at its natural icon size without reserved headroom. Use a taller complete Cooking stove image after adding the pot, keeping the stove body at exactly the same scale. This is an ordinary assembly combination so each stage has its own natural canvas. Keep the original kitchen background (`assets/backgrounds/25.webp`) with its wall hearth as scenery. Removing an interaction hotspot does not require removing the pictured hearth.

The [completed narrative study](../../archive/2026.11.06%20settlement%20study/narrative-study.md) tracks the revision and its evaluation. New artwork is authorized. Replace mismatched backgrounds and labelled placeholders with matching production artwork, keeping exact generation requests and selected masters in the study. Check actual background season, time, construction state and recurring geography across the full sequence. The ripe orchard and early preserves make the opening a loose growing season rather than literal spring. Festival scenes mark autumn; the weather beach moves towards dusk, astronomy takes place at night across the whole landscape, with visible moonlight, stars and a small lighthouse light, and binding returns indoors under oil light. The closing room may follow at dawn. Do not force every capability into a literal single-year timetable.

Use rough, rustic coloured woodcuts with generous natural blank space in playable backgrounds. The noninteractive ending needs no combination area: show the familiar modest room with the full table and chair visible, retaining a moderate patch of floor (roughly the lower quarter) rather than either the original large empty area or a cramped close-up. Preserve the approved medium-wide composition, window and lived-in warmth. Keep the palette close to the original muted room, only a little richer; avoid the intense orange and harsh black of the first saturated revision. Show printed sheets upright and nearly face-on, with a slight natural skew like the other study-page icons. Check Tower and Lighthouse ground contact separately: their foundation shapes need different vertical placements even though the icon canvases match. Keep the opening orchard, cooking clearing, shelter and house on the home bank; the footbridge crosses away from that ground. Preserve this geography so later visits remain coherent.

Soften playable backgrounds with **22% warm cream (`#fffaf0`)**, baked into the artwork once. Preserve the woodcut detail without blur or additional colour grading, and keep item and hotspot artwork at its original strength. This gives the icons more separation from the scenery while retaining the integrated landscape and Water's opening position in the river. The noninteractive final room is exempt from the standard cream treatment: its selected master already has the softer ochre palette, so export it without an additional veil. Apply the treatment to untreated source artwork; never fade an already finished background again. The [UX artwork contract](../../docs/ux.md#item-artwork) governs rendering.

The catalogue icon pairs a timber cottage with a complete apple tree standing in front of it. Show the tree's trunk rooted in the foreground and keep its branches visibly separate from the roof; the tree must not appear to grow out of the house.
