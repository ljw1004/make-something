# Current UX decisions

This is the authoritative record of the interface contract: interaction, presentation behaviour, navigation, accessibility, tutorials, editing and recovery. It describes the intended experience and includes motivations where known. Game syntax and semantics belong to the [Markdown specification](story-format-spec.md); content-writing guidance belongs to the [authoring guide](story-authoring.md). Reusable artwork methods belong in the [icon guide](icon-guide.md); durable intent for an individual story belongs in its `design-brief.md`. Research and construction history belong in the relevant project. Link to those rules rather than maintaining competing definitions here.

## Maintaining this document

- Update this document in the same change whenever a UX decision is adopted, revised or removed, including decisions made in conversation. Record small interaction details and failure/recovery behavior as well as major screens. A decision does not need a code change to belong here.
- State the latest decision directly in its relevant section. Replace or remove superseded text and reconcile any affected sections; do not append a dated amendment, keep rejected alternatives, or accumulate contradictory rules. Organize by the experience, not by implementation date or commit.
- Accompany a decision with its known motivation when it helps explain the tradeoff. Do not invent a rationale or turn an assumption into evidence of comprehension or enjoyment. Unknown motivation can remain unstated.
- Put chronology, commit references, research provenance, implementation/validation records and evidence limits in [working notes](../notes.md) or the [archive](../archive/README.md). Put unresolved proposals and evaluation work in the [backlog](../projects/backlog.md). None belongs here as a historical log.
- Consult this document before changing UX and check the final change against it. An implementation discrepancy does not silently supersede an agreed decision: fix the discrepancy or track it in the backlog. Other documents may explain usage or implementation, but should link here for the current UX contract.

## Overall presentation

Let the [game's philosophy](../README.md#philosophy) guide player and authoring UX.

The graphical player uses a warm paper palette, outlined cards, square hotspot frames, high-contrast partner/drop states, reading dialogs and explicit chapter arrows. Keep scene artwork visible and leave natural working space for the objects. Gameplay panels have no chapter-title overlay or reserved title strip. Construction target pictures belong in authored background art and have no separate tap action. The interface should support recognizing, trying and discovering combinations without requiring an instructional screen or administrative steps. The separate [browser terminals](#browser-terminals) present the text engine as a development demonstration. The [experience criteria](fun-and-evaluation.md) govern claims about whether that succeeds.

## Catalogue and story identity

Each story’s manuscript provides its title, description and cover artwork. Keep those data in the delivered HTML without a separate catalogue fetch. The story’s heading icon still supplies the individual story’s installation artwork, not the carousel title.

- Use **Make Something Worthwhile** as the catalogue's sole heading, with that capitalization and no final period or separate eyebrow title. Use the same name in the catalogue and player browser tabs, aligning the entry pages with the game's philosophy. The former player-page URLs are removed without redirects; the [README](../README.md#run-the-game) owns current route names.
- Use the gold and teal mosaic with a central four-point star as the shared site identity. Export the square [2048 × 2048 master](../icon.jpeg) to [appicon.png](../assets/appicon.png) at 64 × 64 for the graphical game and simulator tabs, and to [homescreen.png](../assets/homescreen.png) as an opaque 1024 × 1024 PNG for the catalogue’s home-screen icon and the native iOS app’s AppIcon asset. Keep the artwork edge to edge so the platform can apply its own corner mask.
- Present one story at a time in a wrapping carousel on the separate catalogue page. Frame each cover with the same sizing and black side gutters as gameplay. Use the story’s declared cover, falling back to chapter one’s background. Place a rounded shadowed bar with a small inset on both sides near mid-screen with left/right controls, the title and a much smaller Start or Continue label with a circled down arrow under the title, with the whole title area as the launch target. Put shadowed white position dots above the bar and the authored description below, in finer type without a text shadow. Use the same subtitle treatment on every cover. Choose black or white according to the contrast across the visible text on the current cover. Behind each visible line, lightly blur the artwork and lift shadows under black type or darken highlights under white type just enough to give at least 4.5:1 contrast. Preserve colours that already meet that threshold in the blurred artwork. Update that choice as the artwork, layout or scroll position changes. If cover pixels cannot be read, use white with a light backdrop blur. On horizontal swipes, move the outgoing and incoming artwork, banner, dots and abstract together with the finger, then settle into place (instantly with reduced motion). Preserve vertical scrolling of long descriptions. Arrow buttons and arrow keys slide the full outgoing and incoming cards in the corresponding direction, using the swipe easing with a 500 ms travel; repeated arrow inputs are queued and focus follows the corresponding control. Reduced motion switches cards immediately. Dots jump straight to their selected card.
- The web carousel shows the curated stories in `stories/` followed by playable contributions from `contrib/`. Each manuscript gets the same cover, navigation and Start or Continue behavior, so players can explore the experimental web edition using the familiar carousel. The native iOS carousel includes only `stories/`.
- Within the curated collection, show manuscripts in [`carouselOrder`](../src/build/catalogue.ts) first and append unlisted stories alphabetically by full manuscript path. Contributions follow in alphabetical path order. Rebuild after changing collection membership or order. Moving a manuscript between collections gives it a new URL and starts fresh progress and drafts.
- Leave the catalogue URL and browser history unchanged when the selected card changes. Remember the selected manuscript path in browser storage, keyed by the catalogue directory. When opening the catalogue, restore that card or fall back to the first card if the path is gone or storage is unavailable. A legacy `?slide=` link can seed the choice only if there is no valid saved card; never generate that query. Story popup Home links remember their story before navigating to the plain catalogue URL, without clearing story progress or drafts.
- Tune the carousel in [`src/web/style.css`](../src/web/style.css) under the `Carousel tuning` comment. `--carousel-title-*`, `--carousel-action-*` (Start/Continue and down arrow) and `--carousel-abstract-*` control type size and weight, with additional shadow settings for the title and action. `--carousel-dot-glow` controls the dots and `--carousel-bar-inset` sets both side gaps. Keep the correction at full strength across each line box. Feather it outward over 8 CSS pixels, including below the last line, clipping it at the edge of a scrolling description. `cqh` values scale with the artwork frame.
- In the native app, installed web app and Vite preview, show **Support** and **Privacy** as underlined links centered near the bottom of each cover. Use the subtitle’s font size and colour for both links, including after a visit. Give each label its own feathered, untinted blur, lighter than the subtitle blur. Keep the lettering free of text shadows. Keep both tap targets above the bottom safe area. The links and blur move with each cover as the carousel slides. Long descriptions scroll above them. Preview links open in a new tab, the native app hands them to iOS, and the installed web app uses the platform's external-link behavior.
- Open the catalogue without a `story` parameter. Request the manuscript and board artwork upon story entry. Resolve `index.html?story=PATH` relative to that HTML page, without adding a `stories/` prefix. Resolve artwork beside the manuscript. Both inputs must be relative file paths and must not contain `..` anywhere, so authors can move the player and story folders together. Home-screen launches preserve the same relative story selection. Catalogue links use explicit `stories/...` paths beneath the root player page, matching development and publication without parent-directory traversal or copied stories. Development serves the editable `-dev.html` templates at the normal page URLs and regenerates the catalogue on reload. The shared development server also serves project drafts at their repository paths, so authors can preview them without a separate server or adding them to the published catalogue.
- Open each story at its own URL. A missing manuscript shows a visible load error with **Try again**; an unknown catalogue hash bookmark shows an explicit unknown-story error. Do not silently substitute another story. The [README](../README.md#run-the-game) owns route syntax and deployment layout.
- Identify browser progress and drafts by the resolved full manuscript URL and keep them independent. The catalogue shows Continue after a successful combination in the first chapter, after reaching a later chapter, or after completion. A first-chapter save that still offers Restart story shows Start, even if items have been moved. Entering from the catalogue uses a clean story URL and bypasses the standalone cover. Its browser history entry records the carousel origin and input modality, including on Back, Forward and reload. The first matching gesture initializes mouse or touch tutorials. Fresh direct and legacy hash-bookmark links keep the cover and Start; saved direct visits resume without inheriting carousel context.
- Swipe up on a cover or activate **Start / Continue** to bring in the active chapter from below. Swipe down on empty scenery or activate **Home** to reverse the motion and return to that same cover. While a chapter introduction, ending, goals reading or creation wonder is open, a swipe down starting anywhere on the dimmed backdrop, including over an inactive item or control, also returns to the cover. The dialog and backdrop leave with the board. Gestures starting inside a reading follow the [reading dismissal rules](#minimal-chapter-flow). Chapter swipes are disabled while a dialog is open. The encyclopedia has its [own gestures](#encyclopedia), and restart confirmation and errors keep their own controls. Ordinary backdrop taps still follow the [chapter reading rules](#minimal-chapter-flow).
- The cover and board follow a touch or mouse drag. On release, project where the swipe would come to a stop using its recent speed and direction. Complete the slide if that point is at least 65 CSS pixels from the starting point along the swipe axis. Use the same threshold in all directions and at every viewport size. A quick flick can travel a short distance and still complete. A short, slow drag returns to its starting position. Pausing before release lets the distance already moved decide. Gestures that start in a scrollable cover description keep its vertical scrolling.
- Swiping into a story resumes the reading open when leaving for the carousel, including its scroll position, or resumes the board if the reading was closed. Each story remembers its own departure. With no remembered departure, swiping **Start** shows the introduction and swiping **Continue** resumes play. Activating **Start / Continue** always shows the current chapter’s introduction or ending and discards any queued wonder popups from the previous visit. The discovery, encyclopedia entry and board progress are retained. On entry, show the reading in its resting position when the incoming board is about halfway visible and ready. Its controls are available while the board settles. A tap runs the same motion over about 320 ms. Direct links, reloads and browser Back/Forward restore the destination directly. Reduced motion commits without a slide.

## Home-screen apps

- In standalone WebKit apps, supply the system `Canvas` colour for a solid status-bar background, following the device's light/dark preference. This addresses the unwanted iOS fade over scenery while keeping the clock and system indicators visible. Apply the treatment to both catalogue and player, including loading/error views. The colour source must not paint over artwork, intercept input or add layout height. Let iOS size its status area and keep controls aligned to the current safe-area insets on rotation; ordinary browser tabs retain their existing appearance.
- Adding the catalogue (`/` or `index.html` beneath the site's hosting directory) to a mobile home screen suggests **Make Something** and the shared catalogue artwork. Adding a story suggests its parsed heading title and first heading image, resolved beside its manuscript. Keep the browser-tab title and favicon described above. These are installation suggestions; the browser and user control the final installed label and appearance.
- Use the currently loaded manuscript, including a saved or live-edited draft, for the story's installation metadata. Use the same artwork file for a story's heading and home-screen icon; do not add a separate home-screen artwork field or generate icon copies or separate manifest files. Creation uses the simplified opaque square mosaic of golden light and a turquoise wave, with broad forms and quiet indigo areas to keep it readable at home-screen size. Its artwork extends to every edge to avoid a white surround; the complete detailed mosaic is retained for the Song of creation item. Other stories retain their authored silhouettes. Keep artwork corners square and let the platform supply its home-screen mask. The specification already requires square heading artwork; an incomplete draft with no usable icon remains playable without a substitute install icon or manifest.
- Keep story URLs and gameplay controls the same in an ordinary tab and an installed view. The [debug controls](#debug-pane) follow the launch context. Do not show a Home icon on the playing surface. A separately pinned story opens directly rather than inheriting the catalogue launch context.

## Native iOS app

Open the installed app at the story carousel. Play uses the bundled production game, with its illustrated stories and encyclopedia readings available from the first launch offline. Progress and the selected story persist within the app between launches. A player who taps an external encyclopedia citation hands that URL to iOS and can return to the same game. Keep system indicators and controls clear of one another in portrait and landscape.

Extend the catalogue and player backgrounds behind the system status bar and home indicator. Use a light, untinted blur to soften sharp details behind the clock and system icons. Taper the blur to zero within the top safe area. Keep the artwork below it clear and the home indicator area clear of controls.

A successful combination gives a light native impact on supported hardware. **Haptics** is on by default. Players can turn it off in **Settings → Apps → Make Something**. Keep this occasional preference in iOS Settings to leave the carousel uncluttered. A change applies to the next combination when play resumes.

## Artwork loading

- On direct story visits, present **Loading story…** as plain, static dark text centered horizontally and vertically on a cream background filling the game pane, matching the catalogue palette. Keep this first impression quiet: no panel, icon, spinner or animation. Include the loading view and its essential styling in the initial HTML so it is present before the application JavaScript loads, and replace it as soon as the opening view is ready without adding a minimum display duration.
- On carousel entry, begin movement and loading together. An unfinished load slides in as a black surface with a small pale spinner after a brief wait. A downward swipe can return to the cover during the wait. Replace the loading view as soon as the starting board is ready. When entry includes an introduction, show it at the midpoint or as soon as the board is ready if it has passed that point. Keep the spinner still with reduced motion. Use the ordinary loading error and retry if entry fails.
- For a fresh story, **Loading story…** lasts only through manuscript preparation and loading/decoding the cover (or the first background when no cover is declared). Item dimensions are not needed for a cover. Restoring saved progress waits only for the current chapter's visible artwork and immediate reading, with scrollback prepared afterward. Resizing must not reveal an unprepared opening view.
- While the cover is visible, preload the first chapter's starting artwork. During play, preload only artwork one available combination away, including its fitted overlays and applicable discovery illustrations. Refresh that set after actions; do not recursively follow the recipe graph. Each chapter also preloads the next chapter's background, hotspots, new primitive supplies, changed icons for carried items and introductory illustrations. Use the current game's supply and combination rules, including exclusions and already completed chapter skips. Resolve item artwork using the chapter being prepared; scrollback uses the current progression chapter and loads its revised artwork after the current view is ready. Unrelated future artwork must not compete with the current view or spend the player's cellular data in advance.
- Visible artwork takes priority over speculative work. Keep downloads bounded, reuse successful loads and cancel obsolete lookahead. Retry intermittent failures with bounded backoff; an exhausted failure keeps readable item/hotspot fallbacks and remains eligible for a later retry, including when connectivity returns.
- If Start, a combination or a chapter jump outruns its preload, retain the current view until the needed artwork has dimensions or reaches its failure fallback. Pause game actions during that wait, showing **Loading artwork…** when it lasts long enough to be noticeable. Editing or another explicit checkpoint invalidates the pending action; an old download must not later apply it to a changed story.
- Authoring diagnostics start when the desktop editor is opened. Placement checking may then load the declared hotspot surfaces it needs; ordinary gameplay must not trigger whole-story artwork loading through hidden diagnostics.

## Work surface and dragging

- Animated WebP files use the ordinary background and icon image paths, dimensions and framing. The browser plays the image’s own timing and repeat count. Preserve connected image nodes through redraws so one-shot images retain their final frame. Author each file at the required canvas size; there is no separate lower-resolution animation or still-poster selection.
- Choose scene framing from the proportions of the artwork and the **actual game pane**. Art at least as tall as **9:16** fills panes at least as tall as **4:5**, including ordinary phone and iPad portrait panes. Scale uniformly, center horizontally and align the top. Crop the sides or bottom as needed. The same proportions yield the same framing at any size. Measure the dynamic viewport, accounting for the available height after browser toolbars. Safe-area insets protect controls.
- Fit the complete image proportionally and center it against black in landscape and nearly square panes, and when the artwork is broader than 9:16. Fitting nearly square panes preserves the lower scene and its fixed targets. Scale cards, item artwork, labels and goals along with the fitted scene, applying the [movable item size cap](#item-artwork). A landscape phone may show a small portrait scene without a rotation lock or instructional overlay.
- Covers and chapters use the same framing rule, so **Start** leaves a shared image unchanged. Authors specify `(center Xpx, Ypx)` and hitbox `(size Wpx, Hpx)` in pixels of the actual background image, measured from its top-left corner, including fractional values. These are background pixels, not CSS pixels. Coordinates stay registered to the complete image through scaling and screen cropping; no separate authoring canvas is declared. This lets authors copy measurements directly from an image editor. The renderer retains its internal 390-unit-wide scene and proportional height for display and saved movable positions. In phone fill mode, keep text labels and touch targets physically readable and text cards sized for the pane width independently of additional scenery enlargement. Do not hide objects or shrink phone text to disguise an overcrowded scene.
- The catalogue at `index.html` uses black outside the centered carousel artwork, just like the game. Only the description scrolls if its content does not fit. Hide the scrollbars on the carousel description and the gameplay viewport without disabling scrolling.
- Lay out visited chapters from left to right, with the latest chapter active at the right edge. Arrivals show the full active chapter. A rightward swipe on empty scenery looks back, and a leftward swipe comes forward, settling one chapter at a time without wrapping. During the slide, the two scenes meet at their artwork edges inside a centered artwork frame, like the carousel. Mouse drags on empty scenery and Left/Right keys also step through visited chapters. Wheel input is reserved for scrolling descriptions and readings. Resizing preserves the viewed chapter position, and opening or closing a reading keeps the view where it was.
- The circular right-arrow button advances once the active chapter’s goals are complete. A leftward swipe from that board can also advance. Slide in the newly reached chapter from the right over about 350 ms. Open its introduction in its final screen position as the slide starts and let it be acknowledged while the board is still moving. Browsing a visited chapter leaves its introduction closed. With reduced motion, jump to the chapter and show its introduction immediately.
- The active chapter owns the goals even while a different panel is in view. Keep the goals or the advance arrow visible and fixed on screen during sideways chapter transitions. Earlier boards still support moving and combining items. Moving an item picked up on the active board toward a side does not change boards.
- Suppress browser text selection, tap highlighting, image/link callouts and selection magnifiers throughout the player, including backgrounds, controls, the encyclopedia index and the mobile chapter picker. Suppress browser double-tap and pinch zoom on these surfaces and in readings, while preserving vertical scrolling and the game's own double-tap actions. Accidental native gestures must not colour the scene, magnify it or interrupt play. Only authored chapter prose (automatic introductions and the reopened goals popup) and wonder prose (creation popup and encyclopedia detail page) support native text selection and copying; illustrations and surrounding controls do not. The desktop authoring editor retains its normal text editing and selection.
- The player never displays focus decorations, regardless of input method. Apply this to the catalogue, game objects, Home/debug controls, readings, encyclopedia and mobile chapter picker: focus alone adds no outline, underline, colour change, name badge, reading cue or partner highlight. The main game requires pointer-driven dragging, so keyboard focus cues do not provide a playable alternative and can appear misleadingly after a mobile tap. Keep ordinary hover, press, drag, partner/drop and goal feedback and permanent link styling. Retain semantic controls and internal focus placement/restoration without adding input-mode tracking. The desktop authoring editor keeps its normal focus indicators, caret and text selection.
- Text-based things are rounded cards; hotspots are fixed square frames. Text slots live inside a named construction, use compact rows, and show named empty-slot hints only when authored. Installed parts are labelled. No result catalogue or click-to-build menu belongs in player mode.
- Hover or press shows compatible existing partners. Keep applicable highlights through a drag, including over rich artwork, and through the accepted double-click interval after release until another target is chosen. Hovering another item or station immediately replaces the previous feedback; the old release timer must not clear the new target. If the pointer remains over the released item, expiry returns it to ordinary hover feedback. Moving into empty space during the release interval retains the released item's feedback until expiry. A second click on the same item must not create an off/on flash between taps. Opening a reader clears ordinary partner feedback. Double-click recognition keeps its own timing independently of hover replacement. A creation drop must look different from an ordinary placement. Do not preview the future result name.
- Partner hints answer **“Will doing a drag between these two things help progress anything?”** Progress includes making something, installing a part, fulfilling a placement goal, or setting up an unfinished recipe or goal by putting an ingredient at its required hotspot. It does not require completing the whole recipe now. For `Dough + Wild starter @ Warm cellar`, Dough and the cellar highlight each other before Wild starter exists; Flour does not highlight the cellar merely because it can eventually become starter.
- Preparatory placement remains useful before a compound has all its fittings, or before a later `next:` placement goal is displayed. Consider unfinished authored recipes and goals, with missing ingredients/fittings allowed when they can still be prepared on a compatible branch. Only highlight existing items and visited locations; do not reveal future results. Exclude permanently unavailable prerequisites, excluded or already-created results, completed goals and placements the item already satisfies. An item already at a hotspot can still highlight it if dropping there performs an available operation.
- Logical play exposes compatible reagents and compound fittings without previewing results. Its two explicit modes choose when that information is shown; detailed play also collapses hotspot preparation into the reaction. See [text adventure and logical playtesting](#text-adventure-and-logical-playtesting) for the interaction and its deliberate placement abstraction.
- A single-item hotspot operation is highlighted when it can execute. Parking its unfinished input there does not satisfy a separate receiving-placement requirement; it needs another recipe or goal to justify a preparatory placement hint.
- Hover and press show a useful drag in either direction; once dragging starts, show only targets useful for that dragged item. Two ingredients that require a hotspot highlight each other only when one can actually be dropped onto the other there. Actual combinations retain their full ingredient, fitting and context checks. Amber drop feedback identifies a creation or useful placement; ordinary repositioning alone does not earn that cue.
- Drop targeting uses the axis-aligned rectangle whose opposite corners are the cursor and the centre of the dragged item's held rectangle, before fitting it back inside the board. A target qualifies when this cursor-to-centre rectangle intersects its hit area, including boundary contact and rectangles collapsed to a horizontal line, vertical line or point. Use measured tile rectangles, excluding floating names, outlines and shadows; fixed stations use their actual circle or rectangle, so a circular station's bounding-box corners do not count. Among targets supporting an available combination or useful placement, prefer those containing the cursor, then the frontmost target within each group. Incompatible items never mask compatible ones, even when the cursor is on the incompatible item. Apply the same selection during preview and release, with amber feedback identifying the selected useful target. If none qualifies, use ordinary placement, including station membership when applicable. This accepts aiming with either the pointer or the object's centre and gives off-centre pickups some forgiveness without letting a distant edge of the artwork trigger an accidental combination.
- A hotspot accepts a contextual combination through its actual occupants. When the hotspot is selected, drag feedback highlights both it and its uniquely selected receiving item as creation targets. A geometrically targeted compatible item can resolve a station's multiple compatible occupants using the drop-target rules above. Without such a target, an ambiguous station leaves the incoming item in place and highlights the alternatives; never choose an occupant by recipe order. The [specification](story-format-spec.md) owns occupant and chapter-surface rules.
- Dragging a movable thing moves it; swiping empty scenery browses chapters or returns to the cover, according to its direction. A gesture starting inside a fixed hotspot’s hit area stays with that hotspot until release or cancellation, even after the pointer leaves it: the hotspot stays fixed and the gesture never scrolls the chapter view. This lets players safely try dragging unfamiliar objects. Recognize travel beyond click tolerance from both move and release coordinates, retaining a movable item’s pickup offset even when a quick release arrives before an intermediate move event. An attempted hotspot drag does not count toward a double-tap, including when it returns to its starting point; keep ordinary press and partner feedback. Small movements within click tolerance remain clicks. Double-tap (or desktop double-click) opens the item’s encyclopedia detail page, even before the goals-screen encyclopedia unlocks; holding never opens one, because a hold can be the prelude to a drag. Keyboard activation remains available. Cancellation, window focus loss, resize and lost pointer capture while a button is still held must not commit an unfinished move or combination. If capture ends after the button is released but before the release event arrives, preserve the gesture so that release completes the drop. Prevent native image dragging from taking over the gesture.
- Decide cross-chapter dragging at pickup and keep that decision for the whole gesture. An item picked up in the active chapter keeps that chapter as its destination and never triggers edge scrolling. While held, its drawing follows the pointer exactly at the original pickup offset, even beyond the page edges. On ordinary release, fit the whole drawing back inside its destination's visible bounds at the nearest valid position; persist that settled position. Select combinations and useful placements from the held geometry before fitting, so snapping back cannot trigger a different action. This preserves the tangible finger/object connection without losing needed pieces. An item picked up in an older chapter may move between visited chapters: holding the pointer in the 55px band at or beyond either side of the centered artwork frame for 250 ms starts continuous scrolling in that direction, including while the pointer stays still. Use the same edge-to-edge artwork spacing as a background swipe. Clip stationary items, labels and hotspots to the visible part of their chapter. Keep the held item and its label together at the pointer. Leaving the edge while carrying an item completes the slide if the board has actually moved at least 65 CSS pixels toward the next board. Use the board’s travel because the edge scroll moves it automatically. The item stays held as the slide completes or returns to its starting chapter. Leaving the edge resets the hold. Release and cancellation stop scrolling. If the player drops the item mid-slide in the fitted frame, center the chapter under the pointer after resolving the drop. Scroll limits remain the first and latest chapters.
- An older item may enter and leave the active chapter during the same drag. Dropping it there makes its next pickup subject to the active-chapter restriction, regardless of where it was first encountered. Accumulating older items there is an accepted tradeoff; when available under the [recovery rule](#minimal-chapter-flow), **Restart chapter** restores the arrival arrangement and removes that accumulated clutter. Fresh arrivals have no scroll-triggering drags among their own items, while background scrolling still lets the player retrieve older objects.
- Drags should be predictable: when no useful target is selected, dropping A onto incompatible B leaves A where it was released, even when their rectangles overlap. Keep the pickup offset while held and fit within ordinary board-edge limits on release; an incompatible item does not send A back to its starting position. A pause before releasing must never be required to commit an ordinary move.
- Keep a stable back-to-front order for movable items. Picking up an item brings it foremost immediately and leaves it there after release; newly created items and new supplies appear foremost. Hover, partner/drop highlights, keyboard focus, reading, redraws and resizing never reorder items. Highlights stay in the highlighted item's layer: if B is partly behind B2, B2 also covers that part of B's combination highlight. Save the order with their placements; chapter restart restores its arrival order. If A covers B, the overlap belongs to A: grab B through an exposed part or move A away. Fixed hotspots remain behind movable items. This makes overlapping drags and subsequent pickups predictable.
- Both text cards and icons use their full measured rectangles when fitting a released tile within its destination panel. Held drawings retain the pickup offset without edge constraints; release fits the drawing back inside, and reloading preserves the settled placement. Resizing temporarily brings otherwise cropped movable items within reach without overwriting their saved scene coordinates; expanding the viewport restores their arrangement unless the player moves them. Measure text wrapping and compound slot rows as part of the card's height, including at narrow screen widths.
- On a successful combination, anchor the result at the drop destination using the inputs' final computed dispositions; see the [result-placement matrix](#result-placement). Animate the birth without forcing a wait. Never duplicate an existing result, including an incorporated part.
- Earlier panels remain in scrollback. Carry uses actual existing objects and their latest positions. The [chapter arrival layout](#chapter-arrival-layout) reconciles objects brought from different chapters while protecting fixed working places and space for new supplies. Ordinary creation, resize, reading and reload preserve settled positions.

## Chapter arrival layout

- Use the same measured layout for icons and text cards, including wrapped labels and compound rows. An icon's arrival footprint includes its transient name badge above the image, measured even while hidden; tall stacks must not fit their pictures by clipping the first name off the screen. Work within the visible panel, preferring space above the measured goals with breathing room above the goals and below new supplies. Do not shrink artwork or text to disguise crowding.
- Debugger checkpoint jumps have no player arrangement to preserve. Prefer rows centred horizontally, with the whole group around the visible page's vertical midpoint, instead of crowding the top. Start new supplies in their own rows below retained items. Prefer 28px between items at scene scale; reduce that spacing before allowing item overlap. Reconcile the whole arrangement around hotspots and fixed authored starts or inherited occupants. If it cannot fit without overlap, use the ordinary supply reservations and overlap fallbacks below, retaining the centred starting preferences. This applies to checkpoint jumps, including the first chapter; ordinary chapter arrivals preserve the player's arrangement as described below.
- Place newly supplied primitives with authored starting centres exactly at those points. These anchors reserve space before automatic placement and may overlap hotspots. Their authored starts apply only on first arrival; subsequently they are ordinary retained objects.
- When a chapter has no explicit background, it inherits the previous background and hotspots. Items already occupying those inherited hotspots retain their exact positions and hotspot membership. Treat them as fixed obstacles for other items. A chapter with an explicit background does not receive this exception.
- Plan a concrete arrangement for all remaining new supplies near the bottom before placing retained objects. Reserve its region and a gap above it. Try alternative row arrangements and higher reservations when stations obstruct the bottom. If a separate lower group cannot fit, pack the supplies into other clear pockets as a group.
- Place retained objects by their last occupied chapter, starting with the previous visited chapter and working backwards. Use their latest coordinates, not their original creation positions. Fixed authored arrangements can already exceed the current allowance; automatic placement must not worsen excess beyond that allowance. Retry the whole movable arrangement as space constraints yield, before gradually increasing item overlap. Even when greater overlap is unavoidable, compare complete arrangements to reduce concealment; never fall back to placing each item independently at the same corner.

Automatic visual overlap never changes hotspot membership, triggers a recipe or satisfies a placement goal; those remain player actions.
- After placing retained objects, lift the planned new supplies to a short distance below them when possible, checking the new positions against hotspots and items again. Do not replay either effect on ordinary redraws or when reloading an already-ready chapter. A new background uses a down arrow; an inherited background uses a right arrow. ### Detailed play

The player decides whether to inspect for compatibility or act on an inference. This makes seeking information an observable decision instead of giving the entire compatible-action menu before every choice. Only `inspect A` and `combine A with B` are gameplay commands; do not display numbered combinations. Inspecting a hotspot lists all available item operands of its reactions. Include currently completable placement goals. No output names or unavailable ingredients are disclosed. With no partners, show `- (none)`.
- If exactly one executable operation involves the inspected object, first show its normal inspection partner list, then `Combined: with B.`, naming the automatically selected partner, then the normal combination report. This explains the implicit action to both humans and AI. For an inspected item used at a hotspot, name the hotspot as the selected partner; for an inspected hotspot, name the selected item. Retain the additional third-ingredient message for contextual reactions. Count that inspection and its automatic result as one decision. Count distinct operations, not distinct partner labels: several reactions at one hotspot do not make inspection automatically choose one.

Prefer an available single-item operation, then the first available contextual reaction in manuscript order, then a placement-only goal. Contextual reactions automatically use an available third ingredient with the required fittings, irrespective of its placement. Print `Combined this together with: B.` before the result. - Report `Created: C.` or `Transformed into: C.`, using the engine's actual input dispositions. Prefix `Consumed: B.` when applicable; name all consumed inputs. Unknown or ambiguous names and malformed commands are errors, change nothing and do not count.

AI uses structured commands, without additional quotes inside the name strings.

Typed letters autocomplete as soon as one current name matches, adding quotes and, after the first combine operand, ` with `. Completion considers every current item and hotspot, even incompatible ones. Keep normal line editing and require Enter to submit the completed command. Piped input accepts the same full commands and `i`/`c` aliases; blank lines look. Other invalid text is an error.
- AI uses `"choice": {"inspect":"A"}` or `"choice": {"combine":"A","with":"B"}` inside its full structured decision reply. ## Result placement

For a two-item combination, A is the dragged item and B is the receiving item, regardless of their order in the Markdown recipe. Use their **final computed dispositions**, after the engine protects ingredients still needed later. A preserved A returns to its exact source position and placement; a preserved B stays put.

| Dragged A | Receiving B | What remains | Where C appears |
|---|---|---|---|
| preserve | preserve | A returns; B stays | At B's corner with a small overlap |
| preserve | transform | A returns; B disappears | Replaces B in place |
| preserve | consume | A returns; B disappears | Occupies B's vacated position |
| transform | preserve | A disappears; B stays | At B's corner with a small overlap |
| transform | transform | Both disappear | Replaces B in place |
| transform | consume | Both disappear | Occupies B's vacated position |
| consume | preserve | A disappears; B stays | At B's corner with a small overlap |
| consume | transform | Both disappear | Replaces B in place |
| consume | consume | Both disappear | Occupies B's vacated position |

Centre a replacement on B's former centre, using the actual dimensions of both tiles and adjusting only enough to keep C on the board.

For an ordinary two-item combination when B survives, try C at its four diagonal corners. Overlap by 10% of the smaller width horizontally and 10% of the smaller height vertically. Use the containing rectangles even for circular objects. This small shared corner keeps the creation visibly connected to its parent while leaving both accessible. Choose a position entirely within the visible board and clear of other available items and present fixed hotspots' measured receiving rectangles, preferring the result centre closest to the usable board centre. This keeps new results from concealing another receiving area when a clear adjacent position is available. If no diagonal qualifies, try the four side-centred positions with the same 10% overlap along the joining axis and the same inward preference. If all eight positions are blocked, fit them within the board and choose the least total intersected area with those same obstacles and B, breaking ties towards the centre.

For a two-item combination that uses a hotspot and keeps B, use the same eight positions around B. Allow overlap with the hotspot used in the combination. Prefer positions that fit naturally on the board, then positions clear of other hotspots within that set. Rank corners and sides together, with a small preference for a corner. The middle 80% of the visible board height carries no edge penalty. Increase the penalty linearly as the artwork or its name enters either outer 10%, so a modest overlap can beat a placement near the edge. Charge for total overlap and for the share of the most covered neighboring item. When a candidate stays within both limits, use candidates that cover at most 20% of the result rectangle in total against other movable items and at most 30% of any one such item. When all candidates exceed a limit, use the overlap and edge costs to choose among them. This lets the result appear beside or below a high station while keeping it close to the combination.

Keep the player's existing placements intact. Reserve top-edge room for the new icon's name without including floating names in the overlap rectangles. Apply these rules to text cards and icons, including drops between visited chapter panels. Logical chapter and hotspot placement follow the [specification](story-format-spec.md).

Compound installation adds the component to the existing compound, which stays in place even when it was the dragged item. A newly created movable result of a single-item hotspot operation is centred on the receiving hotspot, adjusted only enough to fit on the visible board. This keeps the result where the player performed the operation: dragging dough onto an oven leaves bread at the oven. Transformation uses the same receiving-centre rule as other single-item operations. All retain their normal logical receiving chapter and hotspot membership.

An applicable hotspot `place:` instruction takes precedence over the default matrix when an item enters that hotspot on a chapter panel and for newly created results there. Try its exact centre using measured item dimensions after input retirement; occupied or off-board rectangles fall back to the matrix/ordinary drop. Other existing items stay put. An item already occupying that hotspot on the same panel keeps its released position when rearranged, so the player can adjust the initial arrangement. Leaving the hit area and returning during one drag still counts as rearranging; dropping outside and then re-entering can snap again, including after goal completion. Single-item hotspot results without a usable authored destination follow the receiving-centre rule above, using their actual dimensions. Redraws, resizing and reload do not reapply the instruction. Syntax and containment checks belong to the [specification](story-format-spec.md).

## Story analysis comparisons

Comparison-table column titles come from the input paths so arbitrary manuscripts receive compact, recognizable names without story-specific aliases. Use the basename without `.md` when it is unique among the selected inputs. For repeated basenames, prepend only enough parent directories to distinguish each path. Within each repeated-basename group, remove any shared prefix of the leading directory names through a hyphen, underscore, dot or whitespace boundary, provided every directory name stays nonempty. Thus `mars-v8/mars.md` and `mars-v9/mars.md` become `v8/mars` and `v9/mars`; a separately selected `cookbook.md` stays `cookbook`. Keep input order. The [authoring guide](story-authoring.md#analyze-combination-structure) describes commands and counting conventions.

The analyzer requires explicit file operands in every output mode. Resolve them against the current directory, preserving input order and deduplicating resolved paths. Shell globs select groups of files; there is no implicit catalogue selection, story-name lookup or `--include` option. Accept any filename or extension. Because the parser deliberately recovers from malformed input, recognize a story only when it has at least one parsed chapter, at least one combination and at least one goal. All three are required: prose with chapter-like headings, goals without combinations and combinations without goals do not qualify. Existing parse diagnostics and analysis failures still apply. Filename conventions do not affect this decision.

Table mode processes each input independently. Print a warning naming each unreadable, unrecognized or unanalyzable input and its reason to stderr, keeping stdout and saved tables free of warnings. This lets shell globs include companion documents without blocking a comparison. Compute column titles from the surviving stories. Succeed when at least one story remains; if none remain, fail without printing a table or changing an existing output file. Never overwrite any supplied input, including skipped files. Plain-text and JSON modes fail on an input error.

## Persistence and draft recovery

- Save accepted play locally and resume valid progress on reload, including item arrangements, discovery acknowledgments and chapter-restart eligibility. Report save failures visibly without replacing normal play with routine saved-status messages. Tutorial completion follows its [separate reset rules](#contextual-tutorials); a successful game save must not conceal a failed tutorial save.
- A local draft shadows the original manuscript for its URL, including an empty or invalid draft. Save the raw text immediately before parsing, linting or previewing it; these operations must not endanger the author's buffer. Closing the pane, resizing, reloading and restarting a chapter or story keep the draft. Editing is local and nothing uploads automatically.
- Preserve concurrent work from other tabs under **Saved copies**, without replacing the open buffer. Show a notice when another tab saves different text. Each editing session retains its latest saved buffer; offer copies that differ from the open text, including raw recovery data for damaged records. Loading a saved copy first preserves the current buffer and must refuse replacement if that safeguard fails. Keep retained session copies without automatic pruning.
- Show **Delete draft…** only when the open story has a local draft, including edits held in memory after a save failure. Hide it for the original manuscript and again after successful deletion, so the debugger does not offer an action with nothing to delete. The action is separate from closing the editor and restarting gameplay, and requires explicit confirmation. Retrieve the original manuscript before deleting the draft; a failed fetch or a draft changed during retrieval cancels deletion and keeps the text. Successful deletion returns to the original manuscript.
- After a failed draft save, keep the text open, selectable and copyable, show the failure and warn before leaving with unsaved work. Export uses the editor's normal select-all and copy commands, without separate copy/download controls. Browser storage is local, not an uploaded backup; saved copies protect editing sessions but do not survive clearing all browser data. The [README](../README.md#edit-a-story-live) gives the recovery and backup instructions.
