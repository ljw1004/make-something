# Scientific grounding across scales

Author reference for exploring **organelles → cells → tissues → organs and systems**, originally prepared for grades **8–10**. The current grades **9–10, non-AP/non-Honors** audience is governed by the [curriculum audit](../curriculum.md) and design brief; the earlier NGSS comparisons below remain background rather than a ceiling on the current story. The educational opportunity is to explain an ordinary body's work through relationships that reappear at several scales. This is background for design, not a required vocabulary list or an adopted chapter plan. The [design brief](../../../stories/anatomy/design-brief.md) owns the chosen story; [school scope](school-scope.md) records the organ-level prototype's teaching boundary. These notes supply the additional grounding needed if that boundary expands.

The [source index](README.md) owns the existing library's bibliography and licensing. Its college textbook remains author background. The [existing accuracy cautions](reading-guide.md#accuracy-cautions) still apply. No playtest or review judgments inform this reference.

## A school-level explanation at several scales

[NGSS MS-LS1-2](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ls1-2-molecules-organisms-structures-and-processes) emphasizes the whole cell and the contributions of its parts, excluding biochemical mechanisms from assessment. The interacting-system and hierarchical-model expectations already identified in [school scope](school-scope.md#curriculum-grounding) provide the bridge upward. [HS-LS1-3](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/hs-ls1-3-molecules-organisms-structures-and-processes) adds investigation of feedback while excluding its cellular mechanisms from assessment. These support relationships and observable consequences; they do not require reaction chains, an organelle catalogue or clinical physiology.

**Design inference:** reuse one concrete job across scales. A cell makes a material; organized tissue gives that material a useful role; circulation supplies the cells; a movement or repair problem reveals why the whole arrangement matters. Going smaller should answer a question the player already has. It need not make every small component a separate collectible.

| Scale or connection | Sound explanation to build on | Boundary for combinations |
|---|---|---|
| Cell boundary and internal work | A cell membrane separates an inside from its surroundings and controls exchanges. A living cell continually uses materials, maintains its organization and performs work. | A membrane containing a nucleus and mitochondrion is a deliberately incomplete explanatory model, not a recipe that creates life. Cells arise from existing cells. Name supplied cellular machinery where its omission would change the explanation. Human cells have neither chloroplasts nor cell walls. |
| Organelles working together | In a representative protein-secreting cell, instructions from nuclear DNA are used by ribosomes to make proteins. Rough endoplasmic reticulum and the Golgi apparatus help process and route proteins for export; vesicles carry cargo. ATP production supports this work. | Use an actual output, such as a secreted protein, rather than independent organelle badges. Not every protein takes this export route. A nucleus is not a conscious controller or a switch that starts life. Organelles are parts of an already functioning cellular system. |
| Cells becoming specialized | Different cell types have different structures and patterns of gene activity. A muscle cell, lining cell and matrix-producing cell contribute different work. | Adding extra mitochondria does not by itself turn a generic cell into a muscle cell. Specialization involves coordinated changes. An assembly of mature model parts explains organization, not development from an embryo. |
| Cells and extracellular matrix making tissue | Tissues depend on cells, their arrangement and material outside the cells. Fibroblasts produce much connective-tissue matrix, including collagen. Collagen-rich tendon transfers tension; mineralized bone matrix provides a different kind of support. | A tissue is not just a heap of identical cells. Matrix is functional material made and maintained by cells, not decorative glue. A generic tissue token cannot become every organ without specialized arrangements and connections. |
| Muscle cells, tissues and movement | Skeletal muscle fibers are long cells. Organized contractile structures generate tension; connective tissues and tendons transmit it to bones. Nerves coordinate activation, while circulation supplies materials. | A whole muscle is an organ containing several tissues. Contraction means force production, not invariably shortening: muscles can hold a load still or control its lowering while lengthening. Opposing pulls are useful without implying that one muscle is always completely off. |
| Exchange surfaces and supply | Thin alveolar and capillary walls allow oxygen and carbon dioxide to diffuse between air and blood. Ventilation renews air; circulation renews blood and transports substances to and from tissues. | Air space and blood remain separate compartments. Thinness alone does not provide an air route, flowing blood or a maintained gas difference. An alveolus is not a hole leaking air directly into blood. Breathing, gas exchange, circulation and cellular respiration are distinct jobs. |

## Energy without misleading shorthand

ATP is a molecule cells continually regenerate and use to couple energy-releasing processes to work, including movement and building materials. For a school explanation: **cells use nutrients to replenish ATP; mitochondria use oxygen in a major ATP-producing pathway**. Keep the upstream food supply, oxygen delivery and downstream work connected. ATP is not an inexhaustible object obtained once, and the heart does not deliver a central supply of ATP to every organ. Existing local OpenStax [24.1](local/openstax/markdown/24-1-overview-of-metabolic-reactions.md) and [24.2](local/openstax/markdown/24-2-carbohydrate-metabolism.md) provide background, subject to the corrections below.

- **Mitochondria do not supply all ATP.** Glycolysis in the cytosol also makes ATP and does not directly require oxygen. Many cells use both pathways; their contributions vary. A combination may demonstrate mitochondrial support for sustained cellular work, but should not teach “without mitochondria, no ATP exists.” [Mookerjee and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28270511/) measured glycolytic and oxidative contributions in cultured muscle precursor cells. Their numerical calculations have a [published correction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30097494/); no yield or proportion from that study is required here.
- **Oxygen is not energy or food.** In aerobic respiration it accepts electrons at the end of the mitochondrial transport chain. That author-level detail explains why oxygen supply matters; the player need only connect oxygen and nutrients with continued cellular work. Do not write “lungs turn air into energy,” or depict oxygen alone becoming ATP. Avoid teaching that breaking a chemical bond by itself releases energy; the net reaction and its coupling matter.
- **The pathways are not exclusive on/off modes.** Glycolysis operates while oxygen is available too. Lactate is a usable metabolic material, not simply poisonous waste. [Emhoff and colleagues' human exercise study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23788576/) traced lactate oxidation during rest and exercise. Its small adult male sample supports the use of lactate as fuel, not a universal rate for children or all activities.
- **Lactate buildup is not the explanation for next-day soreness.** [Schwane and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27409551/) compared level and downhill running: blood lactate and later soreness did not rise together. Do not turn an anaerobic-energy recipe into a delayed-soreness recipe, or replace it with a claim that all soreness has one completely settled mechanism.
- **Preserve the source corrections.** Historical 24.2 wrongly names lactic acid as the electron acceptor in lactate formation and presents a fixed ATP total. Pyruvate is reduced to lactate while NADH is oxidized, allowing glycolysis to continue. Keep those chemicals in author notes, and omit exact ATP accounting from play. [Existing accuracy cautions](reading-guide.md#accuracy-cautions) own the detailed correction.

## Repair as an application of the same relationships

**Design inference:** a small skin cut can make the scale connections useful a second time. A previously understood lining is breached; blood must remain in vessels; damaged material must be removed; living cells must rebuild a barrier and its support. This is an explanation of repair in a supplied living body, not a wound-treatment simulation. It does not require antibiotics, named clotting factors, immune-cell subclasses or hormone pathways.

| Visible problem | Biological contribution | Misleading conclusion to prevent |
|---|---|---|
| A small damaged vessel leaks | Platelets and a fibrin protein network help form a local plug that limits blood loss. Platelets are cell fragments. | A clot is not replacement skin or healed tissue; red blood cells do not weave the fibrin network. |
| The damaged site needs defense and cleanup | Immune cells help deal with microbes and debris and exchange signals with other cells involved in repair. | Injury is not automatically infection. Inflammation is not synonymous with infection, and immune cooperation is more than destroying enemies. |
| Tissue is missing or disrupted | Fibroblasts lay down connective material; new vessels can supply growing tissue; epithelial cells move and multiply to restore surface coverage. | Collagen alone is not complete skin. White blood cells do not simply turn into skin, and sealing the surface does not mean all rebuilding has finished. |
| The surface closes and strength returns | Matrix organization changes as repair continues. Depending on damage, a scar can remain and differ from the original tissue. | Repair is not universal perfect regeneration. There is no fixed timetable or identical outcome for all tissues and wounds. |

These jobs overlap. A game's sequence may foreground one problem at a time without claiming that clotting must finish before any immune response begins, or that all immune activity stops before new tissue forms. Pinned OpenStax 4.6 supplies an introductory account but compresses the phases and overgeneralizes different injuries; do not copy its absolute claims or treatment advice. Its fibroblast/matrix account should be read with existing [4.3](local/openstax/markdown/4-3-connective-tissue-supports-and-protects.md).

Primary checks make cooperation more than a metaphor. [Mirza, DiPietro and Koh (2009)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19850888/) experimentally depleted macrophages in mice and observed impaired epithelial closure, collagen deposition and vessel formation. This is animal-model evidence, not a human recovery timetable. [Liu and colleagues (2025)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39729995/) followed human skin wounds across repair stages and identified contributions of macrophages and fibroblasts to the movement of surface cells. Their molecular methods and named regulators need not enter the game.

## Homeostasis as continuing work

Homeostasis maintains conditions within useful ranges through ongoing activity. An assembled body still needs supplies, exchange and regulation. For an accessible extension, sustained activity or a warm environment raises a temperature-control problem: sensing warming can increase sweating, and evaporation removes heat. As the disturbance eases, the response can diminish. A single lifted glass need not cause sweating; humid conditions can limit evaporation. Existing OpenStax [1.5](local/openstax/markdown/1-5-homeostasis.md) and [24.6](local/openstax/markdown/24-6-energy-and-heat-balance.md), with [school scope's cooling boundary](school-scope.md#starting-knowledge-and-teaching-boundary), support this example.

**Design inference:** an outcome should show what changed, which response countered it and what remains active afterward. “Add nerves → homeostasis” omits the controlled condition and the response. Healing restores useful structure over time; clotting includes local amplification. Neither is an adequate stand-in for explaining a stabilizing temperature feedback loop. Avoid making the brain the controller of every local cellular response.

## Human, historical and evolutionary connections for author notes

- **An ordinary action has an invisible supply chain.** Holding a glass still can involve continuing tension and ATP use; a visible lack of motion does not mean the cells have stopped working. Rebuilding a small patch of skin also requires ongoing supply and several kinds of cellular work. These connections join familiar experience to the muscle and repair mechanisms above without offering exercise or treatment advice.
- **Scientists discovered routes, not just named parts.** Jamieson and Palade's [1967 pancreatic-cell experiments](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6035647/) used labeled newly made proteins and cell fractionation to investigate transport between intracellular compartments. This is a useful historical model for asking where material goes next. It was research on guinea-pig pancreatic tissue, not a direct experiment on a generic human cell or a claim that every cellular product follows one route.
- **Cells respond to their surroundings.** [Engler and colleagues (2006)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16923388/) found that the stiffness of laboratory growth substrates influenced specialization-related phenotypes in mesenchymal stem cells. This supports treating matrix as an active part of a cell's environment. It does not establish a recipe in which a soft pad makes a complete brain or a hard pad makes bone.
- **Mitochondria connect us to evolutionary history.** Comparative genomic research supports a bacterial ancestry for mitochondria while the closest ancestral relatives remain debated; [Martijn and colleagues (2018)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29695865/) illustrate that distinction. Ancient endosymbiosis concerns evolution across lineages, not the order in which the player assembles a present-day cell model. Do not portray swallowing a modern bacterium as an instant origin of a human cell, or claim that all organelles share that origin.

These are optional connections and sources of authorial curiosity, not prerequisites for the next move. Research on cells or animals establishes particular mechanisms under particular conditions; it does not establish that this game teaches them successfully to students.

### Skin-barrier comparison in the further detailed study

[Potts and Francoeur (1991), *The influence of stratum corneum morphology on water permeability*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2007788/) measured water-vapor transport through mammalian stratum corneum and related its low permeability to the organization of extracellular lipids. The PubMed abstract and bibliographic record were checked on 12 November 2026. Use the limited qualitative relationship in the planned drawings: the cornified layer and its lipids **reduce**, rather than eliminate, water loss. Do not transfer the paper's numerical comparisons to a human-game diagram or treat this as sweat evaporation. The full article was not downloaded. This supplies grounding for the exposed-surface/outer-layer comparison, not evidence about its educational effectiveness.


### Repair strength comparison — verified 12 November 2026

The round-four diagram compares coverage with underlying load-bearing support. [Doillon, Dunn and Silver (1988), “Relationship between mechanical properties and collagen structure of closed and open wounds”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3205021/) reports changing collagen architecture alongside mechanical properties during repair in guinea pigs. This primary abstract supports the qualitative connection, not a numeric human strength curve or a claim that simply adding collagen instantly heals skin. The manuscript labels remodeling as continuing and treats the matched-pull panels as explanatory diagrams, not instructions for handling a wound. Full text was not downloaded. The existing human skin-repair reference and OpenStax cell-production sources remain the broader grounding.

### Full-story feedback and microbial habitat — verified 12 November 2026

The full manuscript uses a heavier cup as a schematic perturbation, with sensory feedback contributing to arm adjustment. [Crago, Houk and Hasan (1976), *Regulatory actions of human stretch reflex*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/978238/) studied load disturbances in human elbow flexors. Its distinction between an initial reflex and later task-dependent responses matters: do not claim that a simple reflex alone restores a chosen joint angle. [Papaioannou and Dimitriou (2021)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33627426/) studied spindle activity and perturbation responses during movement preparation, supporting task-dependent tuning rather than a fixed mechanical servo. The draft's initial attribution of the 1976 paper to Matthews was incorrect; the primary bibliographic record supplies the corrected authors. Neither paper tests the game's exact cup scene or its educational effect.

The colon passage follows fermentation products into the host without claiming that every microbe or product is beneficial. [Boets and colleagues' stable-isotope study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5233652/) administered labelled short-chain fatty acids to the colon of 12 healthy participants and traced their availability and metabolism. It supports uptake and subsequent processing, not an exact human fermentation yield in the game's meal. [Cummings and colleagues (1987)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3678950/) measured these acids in colonic contents and blood and inferred differing uptake along the route, including colonic use of butyrate. The basic colon anatomy, mucus and water recovery remain grounded in pinned OpenStax 23.5. These are limited mechanism sources, not clinical or dietary recommendations.

The kidney candidate also cites [Bishop, Green and Thomas (1979)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/469722/), a rat proximal-tubule micropuncture study of glucose, solute and water recovery. Its species and experimental manipulation are explicit limits; it does not establish quantitative human outcomes. Primary abstracts/bibliographic records were checked; no additional full-text article copies or images were retained.
