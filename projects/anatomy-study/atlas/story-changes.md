# Selected atlas: story corrections

The canonical manuscript now reconstructs physical objects whose parts remain recognizable, with explanations in prose. Chapter 1 retains three actions: an amorphous water sample fills a complete lipid enclosure; tangled chromatin fits inside a double nuclear membrane with pores. Chapter 3 carries the single completed cell forward as the representative for differentiation. Chapter 4 uses a BMP-2 cue with prepared embryonic descendants to construct a bone-building cell, then builds living matrix from that cell and amino acids, mineralizes it once, and uses the resulting material to assemble humerus, ulna, radius, and grouped wrist/hand onto an actual shoulder girdle. Repeated muscle-cell drags then construct biceps and triceps. This is an anatomical reconstruction, not a claim to reproduce embryonic development.

Chapter 8 now ends with a locally constructed ear, with sensory cells physically within the cochlea. Chapter 9 similarly constructs canals and weighted balance sensors; it no longer adds an unexplained brain or route diagram. The finale supplies the later head-scale nerve connections. Its arm handoff now includes the hand and wrist already constructed.

Chapter 18 replaces the generic digestive-enzyme token with amylase, intestinal finishing enzymes, proteases and lipase. Mixing amylase into salivary fluid makes an actual mixture. Amylase yields shorter carbohydrate chains, and the finishing enzymes yield glucose. Bile disperses fat physically; lipase yields fatty acids and monoglycerides. The direct lipase route remains available. Every planned depiction excludes substrate badges, future products, historical reagents, process arrows and detached zooms.

Scientific grounding: OpenStax A&P [bone structure](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/6-3-bone-structure), [bone formation](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/6-4-bone-formation-and-development), [upper-limb bones](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/8-2-bones-of-the-upper-limb), [upper-limb muscles](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/11-5-muscles-of-the-pectoral-girdle-and-upper-limbs), and [chemical digestion](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/23-7-chemical-digestion-and-absorption-a-closer-look). Supporting cartilage, growth, blood supply, ordinary cellular machinery and site-appropriate digestive conditions are included, rather than simulated as separately tested reagents.

The composition review also changes several physical units. Chapter 13 constructs a local lung lobule; chapter 14 supplies the paired lungs at thoracic scale. This is a deliberate change of view, not growth of one literal lobule. Chapter 17 keeps the responding lymph node as the same supported fixed organ instead of overlaying a giant population of isolated immune cells. Chapter 21 retains residual wet food while microbes release fermentation products. Chapter 23 fits the drinking-water sample into the capillary's blood, so the result shows fluid mixing within the vessel.

The finale supplies an explicit Chest region containing the completed breathing apparatus at whole-body scale. It accepts the arm and lymph return without reusing the earlier thoracic-scale fitting canvases. Its ten operations remain.

Representative quantities are intentional: a single cell can become numerous as tissue forms, without a separate multiplication action. That convention does not excuse changing the kind of cell, supplying unexplained specialized descendants, or mismatching the installed scale. The [design brief](../../../stories/anatomy/design-brief.md) owns the story decisions; the [icon guide](../../../docs/icon-guide.md#review-the-construction-not-just-the-cutouts) owns the reusable review method.

The BMP-2 differentiation step is a focused extension beyond core classroom vocabulary. Prior lineage commitment and other normal signals are supplied, rather than claiming that one molecule alone turns any cell into bone. [Wagley et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32535942/) supports BMP-2-associated osteoblast differentiation in human mesenchymal stem cells.
