### Why these disputes changed

The initial relic leads included **Bishop Projectus carrying Stephen’s relics to Tibilis**, [City of God XXII.8](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120122.htm), and **Matrona’s custody and Theodosius’s transfer of John the Baptist’s head**, [Sozomen VII.21](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/26027.htm), which Calvin cites. These are actual source narratives. They were not adopted: using them merely as partial custody records would subordinate their religious meaning to our old puzzle. The new case uses Calvin’s own challenge and lets Trent’s own positive theology determine the reply. The council is not claimed to have adjudicated the Geneva report.

Augustine’s invocation in Sermon 316 is secure textual evidence, but its reference to a *picture* is disputed: it may mean a physical artwork or a rhetorical description. [Appleby, pp.66–67 and n.228](https://escholarship.org/content/qt6fv1d3fn/qt6fv1d3fn.pdf) records both interpretations. The revision therefore uses the invocation and sources its artwork case elsewhere. Likewise, Augustine’s autobiographical food theft in *Confessions* I.19 supplies neither a hungry neighbour nor a promise to return food; the famous pear theft was not driven by need. Merely finding a famous theft would not repair the old example.

Source examples already doing their work remain: Augustine’s wound/healing (*Enchiridion* 11); Aquinas’s fire/water and axe/bed (*Summa* I q.49 a.1 and III q.62 a.1); Zwingli’s ring (*On the Lord’s Supper*, Third Article); Luther’s unbelieving recipient (*Large Catechism* V.16–18, 33–35); the Lyon ritual’s prescription; and the actual Marburg articles. Aquinas himself compares baptismal instrumentality with Eucharistic conversion. No invented historical incidents are needed to replace those examples.

## Themes across all Weakest moments

1. **A result explains the next result in advance.** This appears in 2, 3, 5, 7–12 and 14. 4. **Players disagree about whether a clarification earns its place.** The wound/creation example in 1, final assembly in 5, rites in 7, Marburg comparisons in 11, later deeds in 12 and painting in 13 have mixed assessments. These call for narrow revisions and fresh evidence, rather than deleting every predictable action.

Conversely, the uniformly medium hook in high-Worthwhile chapters 1 and 12 points first to motivation and progression, not wholesale replacement of their comparisons.

Sole-option complaints recur throughout the reports. Compatibility hints are part of normal play, and `--fast` supplies the menu. Adding arbitrary alternatives or hiding hints would not address repeated explanations. First make the existing actions contribute distinct discoveries or valued applications.

## Proposed work on the story's pull

The no-high-hook list is a reason to examine the surrounding arc, not to add cliffhangers to every chapter. Preserve the existing historical movement and keep the player comparing claims rather than choosing which religion to endorse.

- **1–2: carry the same human problem forward.** The wound and stolen meal establish harm, responsibility and a failed intention. Start the second study with the failure to return the meal, then make the grace account answer that problem. Let the infant question be a clearly distinct challenge rather than another generic cleared-desk lesson.
- **4–8: make each change of question explicit.** The fire case has not answered every question about the creator. Introduce the Trinity as a different kind of claim; move from its explanatory limits to bodily rites, then ask why water and bread are given different roles. The instrument comparison is a reason for the transition, not another occasion to repeat the same general caution.
- **9–12: keep the household and the disputed benefit in view.** Use the supper case to make free service matter, then let Marburg and Trent change the question about gift, reception and later deeds. Chapter 11 already demonstrates that a limited agreement can be a satisfying destination. Aim for similar progress in 12 without promising reconciliation.
- **13–14: give the investigation a bounded ending.** The record should change the player’s account of the fragment even though it cannot settle every religious question. End on what became better understood and which disagreement survives; avoid ending with several procedural reminders. High hooks here support retaining the inquiry, not its existing amount of work.
