I'd like you to explore a new story idea. Please create a folder projects/trial-study. This story will be called "On Trial". It will be a set of legal courtroom dramas. In order to fulfill our mandate to "make something worthwhile" they will be significant real-world trials. In order to fulfill our mandate to be a fun combination game, we'll look for Columbo style drama (I guess also "A Few Good Men" or "My Cousin Vinnie" or Hercule Poirot), where one fact "I was at the jewelry store" is combined with another "You said your car was at the garage" to arrive at an interesting combination "Witness is lying". In order to fulfill our target of about 200 combinations, I expect we'll want probably 5 or 6 trials.

Here's my idea of how to make the story worthwhile. We could make the trials tell the story of the relationship between jurisprudence and power over the centuries? How common law developed? How notions of rights arose?

Five Questions
1. Who may judge?
2. What may the law prohibit?
3. What does the evidence establish?
4. When does law become an instrument of injustice?
5. How do we hold those who wield state power accountable?
Most Important Thing for Gameplay

As I said, we want this to become a fun combination game.

What I'd like you to do is research ALL of those trials. For each of them, (1) Find and download what reference material you can, converting to markdown as necessary, into projects/trial-study/references. (2) Look for ten actual moments that are good candidates for combinations -- for instance where a piece of testimony, document, or legal argument changed the significance of another. (3) Write them all up in projects/trial-study/working-notes.md

Before starting, I'd like you to read docs/story-authoring.md to get an idea for what kind of things we look for in a combination game.
