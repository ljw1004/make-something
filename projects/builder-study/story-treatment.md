# The Next Box — a city at the end of a string

Selected narrative treatment, developed under the [single design brief](../../stories/builder/design-brief.md).
The live game remains the preceding v48 implementation. This is the next story's
construction and visual sequence, not a rendered or executable revision.

## Story

The boxes begin with things a child can understand: folded paper, a cord wound
around a reel, a lamp shining through holes. The child gets good at making them.
Then a small wooden sled floats off the table and visits the top bookshelf.
There is no announcement that the rules have changed.

An optical kit reveals a living industrial city above a world that is breaking
apart. The factory there makes the familiar kit boxes. When debris damages its
railway, a worker separates a tow connector into two halves, packs one into a box,
and keeps the other. That box opens on our table.

We build the winch. Then we build the Whale around the actual floating sled and
mount the actual winch inside it. The familiar wooden toy leaves the bedroom,
reaches the city, connects the matching halves and tows the whole city home.
The workers repair their railway. Our Whale returns to its place on the table.
Outside the bedroom, the Moon now has a companion.

The intended feeling is the extraordinary consequence of something made with
one's own hands: this is still our room and our wooden machine, and we have changed
what hangs above the roof. The people's resumed lives give that change substance.

## The party sending the kits

Privately, the supplier is **the Works**, a far-future industrial company operating
an inhabited factory-city. Its old world is failing. Its directors intend to keep
the whole enterprise: the people, houses, railway, workshops, machinery and its
capacity to build a future. They have chosen Earth as the new location and set up
a toy-building programme to get a receiving builder trained and equipped here.

It is a desperate, self-interested relocation. The company has committed to moving
an entire city into our sky without asking its intended neighbours. The early kits
teach the skills its last machines require. The later shipment is a direct request
for an indispensable piece of the operation. The directors' plans and assumptions
stay in the author's understanding; the company has no speech or manifesto.

The visible story must establish the factory as the source, its people's danger,
the deliberate split-connector shipment, the child's necessary intervention, and
the city's permanent arrival. It need not establish every earlier box as part of
an original plan. The transport of the boxes is an accepted story fact. No
explanation of why the company did not send robots belongs in the game.

## The seven constructions

| Build | What the player physically makes | Its contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pinwheel | A connected pre-slit blank, four separate folds, hub, axle and handle. | A quick complete toy, with visible transformation and retention. |
| Marble lift | A broad frame with a reel, guided cord, ratchet, captive lifting cup and return track. | Raising, holding and returning a load; the later winch echoes these visible operations. |
| Star projector | A low enclosure, lamp, gearmotor, pierced disc, spindle and lens. | Real optics, an appealing room-sized result, and the progression from projected stars to the real sky. |
| Float sled | A shallow tray, runners, pink plate, blue battery, handwheel/linkage and guard. | The first private impossible capability; its intact structure becomes the Whale's keel. |
| Sky viewer | A compact optical box, folded hood, mirror, turret and pink lens. | Discovery of the supplier, the endangered city and the request conveyed through hardware. |
| Tow winch | A frame, gearmotor, ratchet, brake, retained rollers and connected pink reel/lead with parked plug. | Controlled deployment, load retention and retrieval; the intact machine later mounts inside the Whale. |
| Whale | The sled, hull seats/panels, hinged steering fins/links, tail, light, winch seats and retaining bridge. | Delivers the plug, supplies flight and tow, then retrieves the connection and returns home. |

Three ordinary whole builds precede four using pink technology. The ordinary
projects earn their places as toys and useful physical ideas; they do not all
become arbitrary ingredients of the finale. Do not pad this scope to the earlier
200-action ambition. Subassembly chapter and action counts come from a construction
prototype, not a target quota.

## Visible sequence

This is a shot and construction treatment. Its descriptive sentences are not
player-facing narration. Normal page text stays a short title, target picture and
current fitting instruction. Pink parts are supplied without comment.

1. PINWHEEL. The player folds four corners of a single connected, pre-slit paper blank, then fits the hub, axle and handle. The blades turn in the garden. The scene establishes the worktable, a familiar bookshelf, a roofline beyond the window and one moon. Wooden kit boxes have a bold three-circle stamp.

2. MARBLE LIFT. A wide base, short supports, crank, reel, ratchet, cord guide, captive cup and enclosed return track form a compact machine. The cup raises a marble, tips it at the top and returns; the marble rolls down the track. A pause shows the ratchet holding the load. The turning reel and moving cord remain visible.

3. STAR PROJECTOR. A blue battery, lamp, small gearmotor, pierced wheel, lens and low enclosure become a projector. Moving stars cross the familiar bookshelf and ceiling. Through the open curtains the real moon remains outside. The next box opens on the same worktable.

4. FLOAT SLED. Fit two runners, a pink plate, blue battery, steering handwheel and its linkage, and a retaining guard to a shallow wooden tray. The finished sled hovers just above the table. It passes the edge, stays in the air and climbs past the bookshelf. It pauses beside an upper-shelf toy, then comes down again. Its wood, runners and red handwheel remain recognizable. This is given its own unhurried scene before opening another box.

5. SKY VIEWER. Build a broad optical box with a folding hood, mirror, lens turret, blue battery and pink lens. It first shows the familiar moon. Turning to another bright point reveals an immense circular city on a rocky platform above a breaking world beneath a red star. A yellow train runs through a white arch with three round openings. Silhouettes move behind large lit windows. The city's factory has open loading bays containing stacks of the same stamped kit boxes seen on the child's table.

A fragment strikes the outer track. A short section breaks away and the yellow train brakes at the gap. Cut close to a factory bench: a worker separates a red plug from its matching socket. The socket stays on the bench. The worker puts the plug, a coiled lead and a pink reel into an ordinary stamped kit box. The lid closes. Match cut to that same lid opening on the child's worktable, with those parts in the same positions. No shipping route is explained.

6. TOW WINCH. The player assembles a wide base, two cheeks, shaft, gearmotor, ratchet, brake, two large guide rollers, blue battery and the supplied pink reel. The reel and lead form one connected supplied part with retained end fittings. The red plug is kept in a fork on the housing. Fit the roller cover to retain the lead and complete the winch. It pays out and retrieves a short glowing length through the rollers; its brake holds during a pause. The housing has two broad mounting feet.

Through the Viewer, the factory workers take the socket kept from the packed pair and bolt it to a ledge beneath the white arch. An incoming fragment passes close to the damaged track. The workers leave the socket clear.

7. WHALE. The actual Float sled returns as the keel, retaining its size, runners and exposed red handwheel. The player fits a broad hull seat, separate curved side panels, hinged steering fins and their links, a short tail and a nose light. A large open belly bay has mounting seats matching the Winch's two feet. Fit the intact Tow winch into those seats and add a large retaining bridge over its housing. The reel remains visible through an open side; its fairlead and parked red plug project below the tail. Both earlier machines are now retained subassemblies in one compact connected craft. The last fitting is real hardware; it completes the Whale and starts its working scene. No connector-on-a-tether dragging, operating recipe or placement on scenery is required.

The completed Whale rises above the worktable and passes the bookshelf. Its wooden runners, red handwheel and winch housing stay visible. It leaves the bedroom window and crosses the familiar roof. The view follows the same craft above the clouds and into space, with Earth receding behind it. Stars briefly stream past the craft; the distant city and red star come into view ahead. A wider view locates the white arch and stopped yellow train, then a much closer ledge view shows the small socket beneath them.

The Whale turns, backs its tail toward the socket and seats the parked red plug. The socket grips the plug; the carrying fork opens. The Whale moves away, nose first, while its reel pays out line through the tail fairlead. The line stays attached to the socket. It straightens. A close view shows the drum take up the last slack, then stop as the ratchet seats and holds it. The Whale leans forward and its pink plate brightens. A wider view follows the taut line to the city, which starts moving clear of the fragment field.

During the return journey the city stays behind the Whale on the same line. The broken world and red star recede. Earth and the familiar moon come into view ahead. The Whale draws the city into position beside that moon, then stops. Only now does the line slacken. At the ledge, the socket opens; the Winch retrieves its red plug into the tail fork. The city remains in position.

A close view returns to the city. Workers lower a short replacement track across the gap. The yellow train crosses it and continues through the white arch, past lit streets and the kit factory. The Whale descends through the clouds, crosses the familiar roof and comes back to the worktable. Its reel and red plug are parked in their original positions.

The final view holds the familiar bookshelf and ordinary toys indoors. Outside, the original moon is still there; beside it is the smaller bright city-disc, with the white arch distinguishable in its silhouette. No congratulation or explanation appears. The journey and arrival use separate short shots with recognition holds, rather than being compressed into a ten-second loop.


## Construction and staging conditions

- Design the sled and winch's physical dimensions first. Their broad mounts and
  unchanged geometry govern the Whale's bay and retaining bridge. Keep the runners,
  red handwheel, reel and line guide exposed enough to recognize. Individual fins,
  links and retainers remain meaningful visible additions.
- The winch's supplied reel/lead is one connected unit with retained fittings and
  a parked plug. It is never a loose screen-spanning cord or a connector that needs
  a new tether-drag interface. Installing the entire winch and its retaining bridge
  is ordinary composition. Deployment, docking and retrieval are use animation.
- The Whale carries the flight capability; the winch pays out, takes up slack,
  holds the line during travel and retrieves it afterward. Show the drum stopped
  under its ratchet during the sustained tow. Pink parts allow the impossible
  reach and load; ordinary gear, fairlead and clip motion must remain consistent.
- The supplied paper is already slit but remains one connected blank. The hood's
  fold is an explicit process. The ordinary projector includes its actual drive.
  The marble cup needs a visible tipping/return mechanism and retained marble path.
- Reserve large readable views for the split connector, matching packing/opening,
  two reused machines and socket engagement. Never solve a scale problem by growing
  the Whale. Locate the ledge in a wide city shot, then cut to its actual small
  interface at a much closer camera distance.
- Follow departure, tow, arrival and return through matched landmarks. Keep the
  city attached until it reaches its new location beside the familiar moon. Show
  repair of the damaged rail and resumed ordinary life before the final room view.
- The final journey is a one-shot narrative use scene with several recognition
  holds, separate from the usual 5–10-second working loops. Determine its duration
  with a rough storyboard at phone size before producing finished artwork.

## Evidence and next production gates

[Four review rounds](narrative-redevelopment.md) include ten fresh blind concept
readers and two fresh informed construction/story reviewers. The final blind pair
both inferred deliberate help requested through the split connector, city rescue,
and a lasting change to the child's sky. They did not establish that every early
kit was preplanned for the rescue; that remains private author intent. One cautioned
that the likely emotional effect is satisfaction and wonder, with limited attachment
to particular inhabitants. That suits the chosen scale; no gratitude exchange is
added to manufacture a personal relationship.

After final review, the sustained tow was clarified to show the ratchet holding a
stationary drum after slack is taken up. This is a narrow motion clarification,
not an additional story beat; it has not received a further independent reading.

These are readings of written scene proposals. They establish neither actual
visual recognition nor child enjoyment, physical fit, force performance or UI
usability. Before implementation expands: prototype the pinwheel and marble
mechanisms, the unchanged sled/winch mounting and their phone-size additions;
make a rough, wordless storyboard of the source reveal, shipment and entire tow;
then give that actual visible material to fresh readers without this treatment.
Require reconstruction of the causal story, not merely recognition of moving
objects. The [brief](../../stories/builder/design-brief.md) continues to govern
later artwork, literal caption audits, executable playtests and physical reviews.
