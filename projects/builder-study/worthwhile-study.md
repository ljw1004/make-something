# Builder: what makes assembly worth doing?

Active experiments under the [single brief](../../stories/builder/design-brief.md).
The user has set aside the future-tech and supplier stories. This is a small-scale
investigation of the work, not development of another overarching narrative.

**Outcome:** this pass found modest, specific benefits from understanding and
completing mechanisms, but did not establish a sustained hook. Three fresh fast
players and one detailed player judged the shortened carrier finale worth doing;
all declined another similar chapter. The wording of the question changed the
reported value on an unchanged chapter. Treat these findings as clues for the next
small experiment, not a recovered full-length game or a rejection of building toys.

The current executable drafts are [Sweeper v2](worthwhile/sweeper-v2.md), two playable
chapters and nine actions, and [Carrier v2](worthwhile/carrier-v2.md), two playable
chapters and eight actions. Both have a real retained subassembly. These are text
prototypes with explicit proposed-picture descriptions, not completed visual games.

## Hypotheses and possible failures

Combinations might earn attention through an anticipated physical result, seeing
how parts enable a mechanism, acquiring a useful capability, making expressive
output or satisfying transformation. A plausible explanation is not proof of any
of these. Casual players can follow every instruction correctly and still find
the whole activity pointless. A worthwhile finished machine does not automatically
make every attachment worth enacting.

The first contrast is a compact crumb sweeper and a snack cable carrier. The former
changes a visible surface and contains a wheel-to-brush transmission; the latter
extends reach through a carrier and drive mechanism. Both have true subassemblies.
Neither starts with a grand plot. Candidate generation also considered a printing
press, music machine, pinball, water lift, shadow theatre and quiet food shuttle.
Music and pinball lose important control/variation when reduced to a fixed loop;
that missing experience must not be credited to our prototype.

The KiwiCo source comparison is retained in the [earlier research](narrative-redevelopment.md#kiwico-reference-research).
Its [lamp development page](https://www.kiwico.com/eureka/lamp) includes a particularly
relevant manufacturer-reported test response: repeated arm construction was tedious,
while seeing the levers move was valued. That anecdote motivates a question about
functional feedback; it is not evidence for our game or for children generally.

## Experiment plan

1. Make a two-chapter sweeper with a retained brush subassembly. Use plain proposed
   diagram descriptions, labeled as word-only evidence, before expensive artwork.
2. Probe one unchanged chapter with three fresh players: usual questions under a
   casual mindset, direct worthwhile/arbitrary questions, and concrete ordinary
   language asking what the player got from doing each step. These are three prompt
   conditions, not a pooled three-player estimate of enjoyment.
3. Select guidance for specific grounded answers rather than favorable scores.
   Play the remaining chapter, revise the project against named observations, and
   retest the changed chapters with new participants.
4. Build and test a different short ordinary invention; revise it and retest.
   Keep mechanics and prompt revisions separately identified. These low-count
   screens generate hypotheses; they do not establish a causal effect or preference
   among real players. Use replication on the most consequential final question.
5. Decide whether the screens justify an actual visual prototype. Text accounts
   of motion are not evidence of the pleasure of seeing a machine work; another
   full artwork pass is not an automatic response to weak assembly. Use detailed
   mode for a focused check of the final sequence.

## Questions and reporting

Exact guidance is frozen in each retained chapter report. All conditions keep the
normal action reasons, expectations, alternatives, effects, reactions and five
closing questions. The casual mindset asks participants to follow instructions
and compatibility mechanically, without inventing clever deliberation. It permits
small pleasures, indifference and satisfaction without treating any as obligatory.

The direct addition asks whether doing each step felt worthwhile, pointless or
arbitrary, mixed, or neither, and why. The concrete addition asks what, if anything,
the player got from doing it and what felt unnecessary. Both ask at chapter end
whether the project itself was worth doing, distinguishing its result from the
process and assigned completion from voluntary continuation.

Keep standard **Worthwhile** and **Fun** counts under the standing rubric. Additional
evidence is separate: project judgment (worth doing / mixed or indifferent / not
worth doing / unclear), grounded positive step reports, arbitrary or unnecessary
step reports, and the actual cited event. A mechanical explanation is not by itself
a personal value claim. Missing evidence remains missing. Look for ritual repetition,
inflated imagined interactivity, retrospective rationalization, and answers that
merely echo the wording of the question. Do not reward negativity for its own sake.

Players use Terra Medium; independent graders and chapter reviewers use Astra High,
unless a run explicitly records a necessary different model. Each chapter/version
has fresh participants and actual shared-engine sessions. Trials, comparisons and
revisions will be attributed below; no scores or outcomes are presumed in this plan.

## Candidate reasoning

These are design hypotheses from the source comparison and a fresh independent
candidate-generation assignment, not player rankings. The assignment received the
ordinary-invention, short-project, real-subassembly and minimal-story constraints;
it preferred a snack gondola and a music machine. The editorial choice of a sweeper
as the first contrast deliberately tests a visible change to an ordinary surface.

| Candidate | What the player could care about | What a fixed assembly game loses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crumb sweeper | A wheel makes another part turn; crumbs actually disappear into a hopper. | Cleaning a chosen surface; repeated useful operation. |
| Snack carrier | Reach across the room; the childish luxury of food arriving by cable. | Choosing the route, cargo and recipient; operating it whenever wanted. |
| Drawing machine | Familiar pieces produce a surprising, accumulating geometric picture. | Choosing settings and keeping one's particular drawing. A fixed example can still be a real spectacle. |
| Music machine | The arrangement of pins becomes an audible tune. | Composition, unless the player really controls the arrangement and hears its result. |
| String-loop shooter | An ordinary loose cord becomes an apparently impossible moving shape. | Aiming, experimenting and feeling the motion. This needs visual evidence before dismissal or acceptance. |
| Printing press | A mechanism makes copies of a mark the player cares about. | Authorship if the print is just another prescribed picture. |
| Pinball | Construction gives access to a recognizable playable toy. | Nearly its entire purpose if the reward is only a demonstration loop. |
| Laundry lift / small inspection rover | Something inconvenient becomes reachable or observable. | Agency if the situation merely supplies an excuse to click through the next kit. |

The distinction to investigate is not useful versus frivolous. A string rising into
the air or a surprising pattern can earn attention without solving a household task.
A practical-sounding sweeper can still be empty work. The question is what the player
gets to **understand, cause, discover or make their own** by doing these combinations.
Those are hypotheses, not four new scoring categories.

## Question-wording trials

An independent protocol-design assignment proposed comparing usual guidance,
direct worthwhile/arbitrary language and concrete “what did you get from doing it?”
questions, with additional signals separate from the standing score. That advice
informed the experiment; it was not a player verdict.

The initial three-way pilot used an unchanged, faulty Sweeper chapter. Its conditional
picture paragraphs were accidentally grouped into one reading, exposing the finished
mechanism after the first fitting. Preserve [v1](worthwhile/sweeper-v1.md) as tested.
This is a same-stimulus **wording pilot**, excluded from comparisons of the intended
assembly sequence. [v1b](worthwhile/sweeper-v1b.md) separates those declarations;
actual shared-engine observations confirmed the future captions no longer appeared.

The direct wording encouraged “worthwhile because it made progress.” The concrete
wording more often identified a specific gear connection or routine second wheel,
but both could rationalize physical necessity. Neither is established superior from
one participant per condition. A corrected chapter was therefore also tried with
direct and concrete wording. A final question revision explicitly separated:

- Was making this one project worth the time: yes, mixed, no or not sure, with an
  experienced moment?
- Hypothetically, keep this assembly, shorten it, or prefer the completed machine;
  what would be lost? This was not an available skip button or observed choice.
- Would the player choose another chapter? Stopping after a satisfying completed
  project does not itself prove the project was worthless.

That final revision, `concrete2`, was screened on unchanged v1b chapter 2 and frozen
for the main comparisons. Its player answered mixed, preferred shortening the
routine fittings, and wanted to retain the gear connection. Exact guidance appears
in each chapter report; use those records rather than this abbreviated description.

## Revisions and observations

The first Sweeper screen found some pleasure in joining parts and understanding
gear contact, alongside repetitive bearings and the second wheel. All players
wanted to stop. [v2](worthwhile/sweeper-v2.md) keeps the same nine actions and adds
two proposed motion moments: the brush rotates inside stationary bearings; a turned
ground wheel drives the brush in the opposite direction. Intermediate motion is an
explicit experimental departure from the brief's normal still subassemblies, not an
adopted animation requirement. New players noticed those
relationships but still reported mixed project value and preferred shorter assembly.
This did not establish increased appetite for the game. Motion was described in
words, not experienced as rendered animation.

The [first carrier](worthwhile/carrier-v1.md) takes twelve actions across Trolley,
Winder and Across the gap. The final loop connection and biscuit journey were valued;
the guide, anchor, repeated wheel and grip fittings supplied little additional
interest. All three players judged their assigned passage mixed and preferred
shortening. The final player's wish to stop after the biscuit arrived is compatible
with a modestly satisfying endpoint; it is not proof that the whole idea failed.
Each participant played one chapter, with earlier actions supplied as checkpoint
history, so these are not three whole-project experiences.

The [shortened carrier](worthwhile/carrier-v2.md) retains the axle-socket join and
two individually fitted trolley wheels. The cord grip becomes an integral connected
part of the tray; the winding station arrives with its clamp, guide and anchor
already attached. The player still fits its crank, far return wheel, support span,
trolley and haul loop. Eight actions replace twelve; two chapters replace three.
This is a coarser assembly granularity, not four physical components disappearing.
It is a multi-change revision, not an isolated test of one removed part.

## Protocol audit and limits

A fresh methods reviewer read only the final guidance and six actual public records,
not manuscripts or previous verdicts. This was an informed audit, not another player.
It found repeated mixed / shorten / stop answers worth a sensitivity check, without
claiming that prompting caused them. Asking an explicitly instruction-following
player what they did “only because the instructions required it” can make compliance
sound self-discrediting. The players usually resisted calling that purposeless.

The audit also identified an overbroad question: one chapter's participant has not
spent the effort of making the whole project. Retained project judgments are
answers to the supplied wording, not whole-project measures. Prior chapters in the
opening history are checkpoint-planner actions. The suggested “hands-on practice”
benefit in one response is also unsupported by this text interface. Neither model
latency nor hypothetical shortening is human effort or observed behavior.

The targeted sensitivity check keeps carrier v1 chapter 3 unchanged, uses a fresh
participant, and explicitly asks about **playing this chapter through to its result**.
It removes the forced shorten/skip comparison, says instruction following is neither
proof of value nor pointlessness, and separates completed experience from desire
for another chapter. Exact replacement guidance is retained with its report. This
changes several related wording features at once; it cannot identify which one
caused any difference. Subsequent detailed and replication checks must identify
which guidance they use and remain separate from the original condition.

The unchanged carrier v1 finale changed from mixed to yes under the alternative
wording, while continuation remained low. That is a sensitivity warning, not proof
that one prompt is correct. Under that same alternative wording, the shortened
carrier finale's three fresh fast participants and one separate detailed participant
all judged their passage worth the effort, and all declined another similar chapter.
The three fast accounts were very similar; freshness does not make them a diverse
sample of children. Their shared final payoff supported 1.7/5 standard Worthwhile
on average. No measured benefit of shortening the **whole** project is established.

A final diagnostic played Creation's first chapter using the same casual mindset
and alternative questions, with only the wording adjusted to truthfully describe an
existing chapter rather than proposed art or a finished mechanical project. The
player valued the asteroid/Moon/tilt relationship and received a medium continuation
judgment, with uncertainty about playing further. This shows the protocol did not
mechanically force every continuation label to low. It is one local control, not a
validated scale of the user's enjoyment, and the changed wording and different
content prevent a controlled story ranking. Creation itself was not edited.

## What this changes in our thinking

1. **A reason for the machine is different from a reason to assemble it.** The
   biscuit journey was a modest valued endpoint. It did not make the preceding
   guide or wheel fittings individually interesting. Conversely, an ordinary
   fitting can carry the value of the visible result it completes.
2. **The strongest clues concern relationships.** Axle and brush turn together
   while bearings stay still; one gear reverses the other; the cable that supports
   the trolley is separate from the loop that moves it. In Creation, a collision
   produces something whose effect changes the world. These are specific things
   a player can newly understand, not just evidence that a part fitted.
3. **More explanation or fewer parts did not recover the larger hook.** The
   Sweeper's added motion descriptions moved attention to those relationships,
   without improving continuation. The shortened carrier made a smaller prototype;
   its tests do not demonstrate a before/after gain in experienced whole-project
   value. Do not optimize the next manuscript to produce flattering yes answers.
4. **Routine and pointless are different.** Most participants found the required
   pieces purposeful even when routine. Their comments about limited choices are
   testimony, not an instruction to remove goals, hide recipes or add arbitrary
   puzzles. The user explicitly wants comprehensible guided construction.

The next candidates should expose a worthwhile behavior as parts combine. Three
concrete directions remain open:

| Next experiment | Two-to-four chapter construction | Observable question | Main failure to test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Drawing engine | Pen carriage, offset crank/linkage, moving paper bed. | How do two simple movements become this particular drawing? Each added linkage changes the marks it can make. | The result could still be someone else's prescribed picture. A real rendered trace, not a written promise of beauty, is essential. |
| Room delivery | Compact carrier, winding station, a real installed route. | What can this newly equipped corner of the room now do? | Our current carrier already shows that a single delivery may be enough. Persistence or control would need to exist in the game before being credited. |
| Under-sofa rover | Steering subassembly, chassis, light/camera module. | What is under there, and can our machine reach it? | An unseen destination could become an arbitrary excuse for another kit. The reveal must matter without a plot lecture. |

The drawing engine is the editorial next hypothesis because its mechanism produces
an accumulating output, and because the user specifically valued seeing a wheel
spin. This is not a player-selected winner and no drawing-engine playtest is claimed.
The rover tests discovery; the room device tests acquired capability. Do not merge
these into a new grand narrative before one earns its assembly.

There is no accepted visual production candidate in this pass. A future test of the
drawing engine needs actual working imagery; the current word-only results cannot
establish that pleasure. The carrier additionally needs an honest solution for a
wide, slender span on a phone. Rendering the entire kit catalogue now would repeat
the earlier mistake of investing before the experience has earned it.

## Execution and verification

Twenty actual shared-engine chapter sessions were completed: nineteen Builder
sessions, including three excluded caption-leak wording pilots, and one Creation
control. All session replays verified. Every player had a fresh independent grader;
every completed chapter/condition had a fresh chapter reviewer. The final fast
carrier condition used three independent players; earlier screens and the detailed
check used one per chapter/condition. Models were Terra Medium for players and Astra
High for grading/review. Capacity was limited to three model jobs at a time alongside
the organizer; authoring and protocol-review assignments also occupied those slots.
Do not pool conditions, versions or modes.

The five prototype manuscripts were linted, and both corrected Sweepers and both
Carriers analyzed to completion. All Sweeper versions have zero lint errors; their
one mixed-fate warning is intentional incorporation of a completed brush. Both
Carriers retain an **incomplete deep lint check**: alternative construction histories
exceed the existing 100-element opportunity-analysis frontier. v1 has two intentional
subassembly mixed-fate warnings; v2 has one. No other lint error was reported. These
are study drafts, not publication-ready manuscripts. The limit was not raised to
obtain a green result. Actual routes complete, but this does not replace exhaustive
opportunity analysis or validate physical dimensions.

To investigate that limit without changing the linter, a separate shared-adventure
check started each whole story in fast mode and recursively executed **every offered
action sequence**, excluding non-action looks, with no state merging. It failed on
any blocked leaf, more than 100,000 visited states or depth above 30. Sweeper v1b and
v2 each completed 144 routes of exactly nine actions (407 visited states each).
Carrier v1 completed 1,152 routes of twelve actions (4,001 states); v2 completed 60
routes of eight actions (206 states). There were no blocked leaves or budget hits.
This checks legal action orderings in this interface, not arbitrary pointer moves,
physical assembly, the linter's separate pessimistic-history analysis or enjoyment.

Public-frame spot checks confirmed that corrected Sweeper and both Carrier drafts
show only currently fitted parts. Geometry, touch usability, art continuity and
motion authenticity remain untested because there is no new rendered art. No engine,
production asset or catalogue build was required. Selected player-response arrays,
chapter reports and mechanically assembled reports are retained below; raw packets,
sessions, transport adapters and grading intermediates were disposable scratch.
