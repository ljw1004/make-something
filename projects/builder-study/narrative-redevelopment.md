# Builder narrative redevelopment

Working story development under the [single brief](../../stories/builder/design-brief.md).
This pass develops the narrative and a suitable construction sequence before
changing the live manuscript or commissioning replacement artwork.

## City-rescue exploration — superseded

The following rounds record an earlier recommendation subsequently rejected by the
user. Their positive concept readings did not establish that the constructions
were worthwhile. The current exploration follows this historical record.

### Assignment and acceptance

The user did not perceive the earlier story or emotional payoff. The next arc
needs a substantial motive for the high-tech kit supplier, consequences that
unfold by the ending, and roughly three or four ordinary contraptions followed by
three or four that use pink technology. Treat extraordinary transport casually;
do not burden a child's story with a justification for why the supplier cannot
send robots. Keep the acquired construction skill, visible composition and
minimal prose. The brief owns the continuing handling and physical-art rules.

Compare genuinely different arcs, then develop alternatives within the strongest
and review the revised concrete sequence again. Blind readers receive only the
proposed visible scenes, object labels and neutral game context. They do not
receive supplier motives, desired interpretations, earlier criticism or other
reviews. Each assignment uses a fresh agent with no inherited history. An
informed construction/motive review is a distinct assignment. These are concept
readings of written scene descriptions, not rendered-art tests, gameplay tests,
or evidence of child enjoyment. Retain concise results and decisions here; raw
packets and individual replies are disposable under `runs/builder-arc/`.

A successful candidate must let a reader identify the main event and the late
machines' causal roles. Corporate history and exact motives may stay partly
implicit, but the author must know them. Recognizing movements or objects alone
is insufficient evidence of a story. The final scene needs a consequence beyond
an invitation to imagine that something important might happen later.

## Iterations

Round one compares a threatened inhabited world's relocation, sunlight extraction
for a dark city, and a self-expanding manufacturing network. All three begin with
the same four ordinary toys, then use four high-tech builds. Readers independently
recount the apparent plot and supplier motive, identify weak/removable builds,
and rank the concrete sequences. No candidate is identified as preferred.

All three round-one readers ranked relocation first, light extraction second,
replicating manufacture third. They inferred a rescue purpose from the damaged
world and the city in the final sky. They independently flagged the dock's unclear
remote effect and overlap between lifting, towing and hauling. Two found that
pre-existing large tow vessels weakened the player's contribution. The light
story's local darkness could read as ordinary sunset; the factory story produced
more factories without a sufficiently consequential destination. The next round
keeps the endangered-city direction but tests substantially different transport
and ending structures.

## Inspiration: Explorers (1985)

The user introduced this film during the second-round design. Screenwriter Eric
Luke describes its origin in his childhood spaceship make-believe and the sense
that early computers might enable extraordinary things through the right program.
That is a creator's account, not an audience study. [Eric Luke interview, 2016](https://podcastingthemsoftly.com/2016/10/13/the-stuff-that-dreams-are-made-of-remembering-explorers-with-eric-luke-by-kent-hill/).
The boys experiment secretly near home and fly around their town before their
space journey. [AFI synopsis](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/57676-EXPLORERS).

Design interpretation: the childhood attraction lies partly in tangible ownership
of a seemingly impossible capability. Familiar rooms and rooftops establish what
has changed. Homemade materials keep the achievement attached to the maker. Local
play and small discoveries give the new capability a life before a mission uses
it. Mystery can invite confidence and exploration without a theory of transport.
These are author inferences from the film accounts, not measured audience findings.

The revised concepts therefore give the first pink toy an unhurried flight past
the child's bookshelf before revealing the endangered city. That same intact
wooden toy later becomes the final vehicle's keel. This preserves a personal
construction history and lets the fantastical scale grow from familiar objects.

## Round two

Three new blind readers receive only the shared ordinary opening and alternative
visible sequences: K, a tethered city relocation ending with a new moon; L, a
miniature civilization transported in a glass garden; M, a beacon and pilot craft
guiding an already powered city through an opening in the sky. All retain four
ordinary builds and four high-tech builds. Readers infer supplier purpose, identify
the player's contribution and missing causal links, and compare concrete endings.

Two readers ranked K first; one ranked L first. All placed M last. K's line,
connector and winch made the player's intervention concrete; L's physical loading
was clear but its habitat and flight were less necessary to the stated danger.
M's beacon shifted from signalling to opening a passage, and the city could
apparently travel without the player's final craft. Select K for the scale of its
consequence, practical connection and return to the familiar bedroom. The minority
preference for L is retained: that reader valued its intimate miniature-world
fantasy and more immediately legible rescue logistics.

## Round three

Two fresh blind readers received a complete visible eight-build K sequence; a
separate fresh informed reviewer received that sequence plus the actual supplier
motive and current construction constraints. The blind readers both reconstructed
rescue and a new moon, but disagreed about readiness. One found no necessary
story repair. The other found the city's danger too adjacent, the workers'
coordination implicit and both interstellar transitions dependent on cuts. The
informed review supported the substantive motive and ending, but required arrival
before cable release, real sled mounts and a credible final connector interaction.

Adopted revisions:

- A fragment directly damages the railway. The threat has an observable effect.
- A worker separates the connector pair, packs the plug/reel and keeps the socket.
  A box-closing/opening match cut puts the same parts on our worktable. The sender
  initiates a concrete request through supplied hardware.
- Departure, Earth receding, the distant approach, continuous towing back to Earth,
  release after arrival and the return home are separate recognizable actions.
- The entire Winch mounts inside the Whale, through broad feet and a real retaining
  bridge. The result is a compact connected craft; no two-machine tabletop tether
  interaction or new draggable-cable interface is needed.
- Retain the single connected pre-slit paper blank and include the ordinary
  projector's actual gearmotor. The Whale retains positive seats for the earlier
  sled and winch at their unchanged dimensions.
- Reduce the ordinary opening to Pinwheel, Marble lift and Star projector. The
  Rover remains a worthwhile toy in the preceding game, but is not essential to
  this shorter narrative. The selected target is now three ordinary plus four
  high-tech whole builds. No replacement filler is added.

Reject the suggested extra operate/pull action as a repair for agency. The user's
selected activity is construction; completing genuinely useful hardware triggers
its working result. The final bridge is a real retaining addition.

## Round four and selection

Two new blind readers received only the revised seven-build visible sequence. Both
reconstructed kit origin, deliberate help through the split connector, the tug's
assembly from prior creations, city relocation and the changed bedroom sky. Both
found a meaningful causal arc. Neither reliably inferred that the original early
kits were already part of a planned evacuation; that stays author intent. One
explicitly expected satisfaction and wonder more than deep attachment to individual
inhabitants. Do not turn this into a claim that a particular emotional response has
been demonstrated in children.

The normal agent thread limit was reached, so the final informed review used a
new ephemeral Codex CLI session, without inherited history, in a read-only context.
Only the current visible and informed packets were supplied verbatim. The default
CLI model was gpt-6.1-sol. It found no concept blocker, accepted the supplier's
substantial self-preservation motive and the compact retained subassemblies, and
identified real dimensioning, fitting access and recognition as production gates.
The other eleven participants were fresh no-history sessions using the parent
model; none was reused for a revision or shown earlier findings.

A final blind reader correctly distinguished taking up slack from sustained towing.
The historical treatment shows the drum take up slack, then stop under its
ratchet while the flight plate supplies movement. Retrieval remains a separate
visible action. This narrow clarification introduces no new story event and has
not received another independent reading.

The then-selected [city-rescue treatment](city-rescue-treatment.md) was the design
deliverable. At that time the single brief named it as the next target, distinct
from the still-live v48 game; that target is superseded below. Four rounds
comprised ten blind concept readers and two informed story/construction reviewers.
These are written-scene assessments, not `--fast`/`--detailed` gameplay trials,
rendered-image reviews, measured emotion, or physical validation. No replacement
manuscript, animation or artwork is claimed. The next evidence must be actual
wordless storyboard recognition and a construction prototype, especially the two
retained machines in the Whale. Raw packets, CLI output and rejected sequences
are removed after recording these concise outcomes.

## Functional appeal and the supplier reconsidered

The user rejected the Whale and questioned whether many proposed constructions
were worthwhile in themselves. They then rejected the city-rescue story and asked
for sinister or recklessly playful motives, a concrete late consequence and a final
build that puts it right. The city-rescue recommendation above is historical, not
an acceptance that survived this feedback. Existing models do not protect objects

### Psychonauts 2 as a narrative question

During this pass the user asked why its sinister forces, reveal and resolution
worked so well. The creator explicitly links Raz's family history and the history
of the institution; the official story setup couples the mole investigation,
Maligula and the family curse. [Tim Schafer interview](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2021/08/26/getting-psyched-for-psychonauts-2-with-tim-schafer/amp/),
[Double Fine story announcement](https://www.doublefine.com/news/behold-the-psychonauts-2-story-trailer).
Plot verification used a [spoiler account](https://www.gamerevolution.com/originals/690770-psychonauts-2-ending-explained-story-spoilers).

Our interpretation: the reveal reorganizes familiar relationships and earlier
helpful actions; the resolution addresses the resulting personal history as well
as the immediate danger. A sinister agent's concrete desire can coexist with
complexity in the person presented as the monster. This is narrative analysis,
not a claim about all players or an instruction to import that game's plot.

For Builder, test one recognizable agent whose earlier help takes on a second
meaning. Let the final construction act on the relationship and machinery already
established. A late accident and a matching cure alone do not supply that structure.
Keep the earlier pleasure real: the player's curiosity is not the mistake.

### New round one: competing motives

Three fresh no-history brainstormers separately developed a possessive collector,
cosmic stunt skaters and a water-park operator stealing a sea. They received the
audience, approximate ordinary/pink build split, literal compact construction,
minimal prose, intrinsic play requirement, late consequence and final repair.
These were invention assignments, not independent approvals.

The organizer also developed a paired-window collector variant. A fresh blind
reader received neutral visible accounts of the rail collector, zipper stunt
course and paired-window collector, without supplier briefs or desired rankings.
It ranked stunt course first, paired-window collector second and rail collector
third. It found the zipper cause/repair clearest, while the friendly hand becoming
a collector's instrument gave the paired-window version a stronger personal
reversal. It flagged unearned scale changes and the unexplained liberation of
other rooms. Its preference for the stunt course remains contrary evidence to the
eventual editorial recommendation.

A separate fresh blind reader received only the water-park visible sequence.
It inferred exploitative collection from the emptied bay, elevated reservoir and
ticket gates. It found the surf toy's final wave effective as a proposed image,
but could not firmly identify the operator as the kit supplier. The gate-lifting
waterwheel's actual linkage and start-up needed repair; the final machine risked
being useful mainly to the mission.

### New round two: trust and possession

A fourth fresh development agent generated three collector variants: helpful hand
as acquisitive owner, endless enforced performance, and worlds folded into perfect
storage. It received the current collector premise, construction constraints and
the hypothesis about reinterpretation. This was an informed creative assignment,
not a blind test. It proposed a workshop robot that physically takes over the
earlier hand, instead of another towing machine or a magical antidote.

A fresh blind reader received the organizer's complete seven-build collector
sequence, with a display carousel as build six. It reconstructed the theft and
counteraction, identified repairing the hand as the strongest retrospective turn,
and distinguished collecting from the unproven motive of stealing credit. It
rejected the carousel's independent appeal and found the robot's support/release
mechanism underspecified. It also warned that prescribed builds and working loops
do not establish attachment. Keep that limit; do not add a claim of player-authored
designs or invent interactive play that the engine lacks.

### New round three: worthwhile sixth build and concrete recovery

Replace the carousel with a working Marble skyway. The helper steals a worthwhile
completed collection; the construction itself does not malfunction. Another fresh
blind reader received this revised visible sequence, excluding earlier feedback
and the private supplier motive. It found the marked finger and careful shelving
made the betrayal recognizable. It accepted the proposed toys' observable appeal
as concept potential. It rejected the inference that a house diagram proves an
actual house is next, and identified a loaded shelf that could not fit the stated
small hatch, plus one replacement motor apparently commanding several axes.

An independent informed reviewer then received corrected opening/tray envelopes,
a continuously supported shelf, distinct service sockets, independent robot power
and the proposed shutter closure. It accepted the physical outline conditionally,
not as geometric validation. It removed an unnecessary wrist operation and a
duplicated drive disconnection, and required visible ratchet release, separate
power boundaries and a clear doorway before closure. It preferred a removable
brace with captive pins over a long loose-pin grille assembly. These corrections
are incorporated in the treatment; no further blind test of that exact final
staging or rendered evidence is claimed.

### Recommendation and evidence limits

Recommend [The Helping Hand](story-treatment.md) for the next visual prototype:
Pinball, Printing press, Mechanical hand, Window tunnels, Wall walker, Marble
skyway and Workshop robot. This choice is editorial, not a unanimous review
result. The stunt-course alternative was the first comparison reader's preference.
The collector better supports the requested investigation of trust reinterpreted
by a sinister reveal; its larger agenda is still not demonstrated visually.
