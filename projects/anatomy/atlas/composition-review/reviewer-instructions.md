# Independent chapter composition review

You are a fresh reviewer of ONE chapter in an anatomy construction game for roughly grades 9–10, illustrated as a naturalistic 1800s anatomical atlas. You did not make these images. Review only the provided frozen packet; do not read previous reviews, project working notes, other chapters or the live production scripts. Do not edit artwork or story.

1. First inspect `inventory.jpg`. Briefly state what construction sequence you intuit from the pictures. This is an inventory with labels, not a blind recognition test; normalized thumbnail sizes are not evidence of installed scale.
2. Then read `recipes.md` and inspect EVERY `operation-NN.png`. These strips show actual operands and results at the same source/4 playing scale, including already fitted parts. Use individual frozen assets from `packet.json` if a detail needs closer inspection. The sequence is a legal reference route, not the only permitted order; the authored combinations include alternatives.
3. Judge the chapter by all three questions below. Give concrete visible evidence, not reassurance or a generic list of anatomical facts.

**A. Components:** Are these sensible physical components that exist as fathomable units? Distinguish a real piece, material sample, supported cutaway or prepared tissue from an arbitrary fragment, decorative token, unexplained scaffold, or something supplied with future anatomy already in it. Names and prose may explain legitimate nonvisible distinctions; they cannot rescue a visibly nonsensical thing.

**B. Composition:** Given A + B → C, does C visually look like those components composed or physically transformed? Check identity, material, amount, scale, orientation, attachment, interior/exterior location, support and persistence of earlier parts. A chemical or developmental transformation need not look like pasted inputs; it must show a sensible material result. For fittings, a coherent receiving site and a believable integrated relationship matter more than exact pixel reuse. Matching canvas dimensions or touching edges is not proof of physical continuity. Flag unexplained magnification changes, missing or duplicated ingredients, whole organs appearing from nowhere, and approximate collage joins that do not form a believable whole.

**C. Standalone result:** Hide the recipe mentally. Does the result look natural in its own right—like the relevant anatomy in a body, or a complete, coherent anatomical cutaway suitable for a doctor's-office poster? Or does it look like unrelated fragments glommed together? Judge organization, proportion, topology, physical boundaries and anatomical plausibility. A neat engraving style and lack of detached insets do not establish this. A useful diagram/cutaway may deliberately exaggerate scale; its whole must still be harmonious and intelligible. Do not demand literal scale between atoms and organs. Do not solve a broken assembly by adding unrelated explanatory badges or detached magnified copies.

**Representative quantities are allowed (synecdoche).** A single cell or part may stand for a population or whole, and a whole may stand for its relevant part. Growth, multiplication and assembly may turn one representative into numerous instances without an extra action. Do not flag count changes alone, or demand literal quantitative conservation from a material supply. Distinguish these intentional simplifications from changes in cellular proportions, material identity, disconnected attachments or an unnatural whole. Prefer continuity with an already constructed representative when a new cluster adds no explanatory value.

Report every serious problem you can substantiate, and identify acceptable constructions too. Do not assume every chapter must fail. Distinguish a questionable anatomical fact from a visible compositional defect. If you rely on uncertain specialist anatomy, verify with primary educational/scientific sources and cite the relevant source in your reasoning. You may conclude “uncertain”; do not certify accuracy you cannot judge.

Return the requested structured result. For each operation give accept/revise/uncertain with a short visible observation. For each issue name the exact objects, state the visible problem, and suggest the smallest adequate repair: layout/registration, coherent whole-and-parts redraw, or revised physical units/combination. Prefer a whole-family redraw when moving cutouts cannot produce natural anatomy. This assignment ends after this chapter; never review its revision or another chapter in this session.
