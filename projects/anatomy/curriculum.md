# Anatomy — ordinary-school curriculum grounding and knowledge audit

This document audits **28 playable chapters and a text ending (chapter 29)** for **grades 9–10, non-AP and non-Honors**, taking ordinary biology. Chapters **1–27 examine local cells, tissues, organs and systems**; chapter **28 assembles one anatomical model** from that work and explicitly supplied surrounding anatomy. The [design brief](design-brief.md) owns the audience, entry assumptions and bounded exceptions, including the user-requested embryonic example. The audit follows actual titles, picture descriptions, recipes and readings, including material supplied within a cell or organ. A broad curriculum heading does not establish familiarity with all its mechanisms.

The [current chapter/illustration arc](working-notes.md#textbook-spread-audit) records the local-chapters/final-assembly structure; the [textbook-spread audit](working-notes.md#textbook-spread-audit) records the latest revision, validation and playtest evidence. This curriculum audit assigns no new playtest scores.

The entry assumptions are cells, membranes and nuclei; DNA as inherited instructions; food as material and energy; and familiar organ jobs. Curriculum inclusion supports a possible teaching destination, **not presumed prior knowledge**. NGSS covers grade bands, the Pennsylvania source is a historical ordinary-course assessment, and GCSE is a different national qualification. None establishes what every American ninth- or tenth-grader knows.

## Classification and current scope

**O — ordinary-course relationship:** explicitly supported at the selected level by the ordinary-course sources. It still needs an introduction in play.

**L — locally taught anatomy or application:** a concrete structure, route or short application explained by this chapter. Its precise name may be new. A renamed molecular sequence does not qualify as L merely because its nouns are plain English.

**D — bounded advanced-mechanism detour:** a new specialized causal mechanism or control comparison, counted against the brief's explicit allocation. Accuracy and a clear picture do not establish that the detour earns its time; matching ordinary-audience Fun, worthwhile and hook evidence remains necessary.

Count introductions, goals, titles, automatic result prose and all braced picture descriptions. **Pictures display automatically on either side of `---`.** Moving a picture after that divider does not make its teaching optional. Prose after the divider can be optional, but its teaching demand still needs an honest record. Author notes do not teach players.

The manuscript retains **four mechanism detours**: nerve-channel propagation (5), tissue-fluid return (16), opposed blood-sugar signals (20) and mechanical balance (9). It now also has the **user-requested named embryonic examples (3 and 5)**, conservatively recorded as a fifth bounded exception because its specific source–protein–recipient relationships go beyond the ordinary-course evidence. This does not authorize more pathways. The former skin-vessel widening/narrowing comparison remains removed; chapter 24 keeps sweat evaporation and shivering. Its supplied vessels teach their ordinary tissue-supply role; the narrowed injured vessel pictured in chapter 25 is a local injury detail, not a restored thermoregulatory control sequence.

Chapters 1–2 keep RNA inside the nucleus/cell and use installed ribosomes there; no standalone RNA or RNA–ribosome complex is made. Chapter 3 retains “same DNA” and explicitly visits two stages of embryonic limb development. Chapter 4 first uses BMP-2 with prepared embryonic mesenchymal descendants to develop bone-building cells, then returns to the anatomical model, builds living bone tissue and then the humerus, ulna, radius and grouped wrist/hand. One earlier Muscle cells supply builds biceps first, then triceps at their real attachments; ordinary growth, joints and supporting tissues are included. Chapter 5 follows other groups of embryonic cells into whole neurons and separate insulating cells, then fits channels into the neuron membrane and wraps around its extension. Detached axons/branches and separate regional muscle-cell identities are removed. Chapter 6 joins actual sensory and motor roots into one spinal nerve. The named developmental topic recurs in chapters 3 and 5; its extra cell/source terminology is audited explicitly, without adding an intracellular pathway catalogue.

Chapter 27's automatic marrow picture glosses “stem cells”: some stay while descendants become blood cells. Chapter 28 uses the previously introduced structures and locally shown spatial relationships. It adds no biochemical, channel or hormonal mechanism. Its supplied model-scale handoffs must not be credited as new organ formation, growth of an entire skin from a sample, or automatically preserved nested compound state.
