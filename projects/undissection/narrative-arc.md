# Lift a glass — narrative arc

This is the current grades 8–10 arc. The [design brief](design-brief.md) owns its scope and scientific abstractions; the [working notes](working-notes.md) preserve the earlier water-balance study and the reason for replacing it.

## Opening question

A hand lifts a glass. What does the rest of the body contribute? The player starts with familiar structures and constructs the connections that make this ordinary action possible. The ending reduces the assembled systems to one body doing something recognizable.

| Chapter | Question and construction | Discovery to earn | Failure to watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| A muscle can only pull | Join bones at an elbow, then fit muscles on each side in either order. | A joint permits movement; muscles supply pulls. Opposing pulls allow active bending and straightening. | Three fittings could feel like labels unless the second direction changes the player's explanation of movement. |
| Where should the hand go? | Connect brain/spinal cord, nerves and eyes to the arm. | Knowing where to reach and producing the force are different jobs. Messages coordinate pulls. | A forced chain of familiar organ connections may be a low-value entrance fee. |
| Oxygen has to reach the arm | Build the blood circuit and breathing apparatus in either order, then connect them. | Moving air and moving blood are different jobs. Oxygen reaches distant tissues, and carbon dioxide returns to the lungs. | Parallel order alone is not a meaningful decision; the convergence must contribute understanding. |
| More than an arm | Break down and absorb food, connect this supply to circulation, and join supply with the earlier controlled arm. Lift the glass and discover that holding it still also takes effort. | Food in the gut is not yet available to muscle. Blood brings supplies; nerves bring instructions. The visible action depends on the rest of the body and the outside world. | The final body could be only a renamed bundle; avoid an unearned claim to reconstruct complete anatomy. |

## Shape and meaning

Each chapter starts with a few parts, makes an intermediate system and reduces its inventory. The final chapter brings earlier systems together and ends with one body. This is a modest test of the user's “few → more complex → one” pleasure, not an implementation of every anatomical part or a simulated living organism.

The worthwhile proposition is that an apparently local action depends on relationships throughout us. The hand is where we see the result; muscles, information, transport, food and air explain why it can keep working. This should be discovered in the joins, not merely asserted in the final paragraph.

The hook is whether making an ordinary action understandable becomes satisfying. No emergency, resurrection, invisible timer or manufactured failure supplies urgency. The combinations and their results must earn interest on their own.

Homeostasis remains useful research and a possible later direction. It is not the organizing principle of this prototype: adding kidney or temperature mechanisms would enlarge the syllabus without helping explain this particular action. Likewise, taking a drink is outside the ending; the glass gives the reach a concrete target.

## Review and acceptance

Fresh reviewers assess the mandatory player-visible explanations, not just the accuracy of optional reference material. They check whether the ideas need specialist knowledge, whether the final act depends intelligibly on earlier constructions and whether the body remains scientifically recognizable. Fresh Terra chapter playtests assess experienced value and desire to continue under the requested `--fast` condition. Human student comprehension and enjoyment remain untested.
