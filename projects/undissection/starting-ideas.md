# Starting ideas and provenance

## The user's direction

The worthwhile question is what we learn about the world and humans' place in it. It is not enough that the real-world activity being represented would be worthwhile. Anatomy and physiology offer a promising subject because familiar human acts depend on unfamiliar relationships within us.

**Undissection** reverses dissection: bring separate parts into relationships until a functioning body emerges. The user suggested both many parts converging into one and a few basic things first proliferating into more complex things before converging. Preserve the satisfaction of construction found in Settlement and Creation without assuming that copying their progression guarantees their appeal.

Homeostasis can supply concrete purposes, such as regulating temperature. Structural assembly should reveal physiology: circulation delivers oxygen and nutrients; metabolism produces wastes and heat; removal and regulatory feedback let the system continue. A final whole should do something, rather than merely have all its labels attached.

The user favours a modern account but is open to historical approaches (humours, Harvey, Bernard/Cannon), different animals such as frogs and pigs, and precedents in medical teaching. The intended players are grades 10–12 students who have studied biology. The user's anecdote about failing anatomy and physiology despite otherwise excellent academic results establishes personal interest, not evidence of any particular learning deficit.

## Possibilities worth comparing

1. **Few → many → one.** Reuse a small vocabulary of living tissues to build structures, connect structures into systems, then let the systems sustain one body.
2. **Make an inside.** Boundaries, selective exchange and an internal fluid environment explain why merely swallowing food or filling lungs does not deliver anything to distant cells.
3. **A body that answers back.** Assemble circulation, ventilation, heat exchange and sensors; a change in surroundings reveals the difference between a working mechanism and a regulated one.
4. **Four tissues, many jobs.** Epithelial, connective, muscle and nervous tissues recur in different proportions and arrangements. Avoid treating them as interchangeable Lego or complete two-ingredient organ recipes.
5. **An ordinary minute.** Standing, walking, drinking or speaking brings previously separate systems into one recognizable act. The ordinary act must earn the preparatory work.
6. **Relatives.** Compare homologous structures or contrasting physiological solutions in other animals. Evolution explains shared plans and compromises; it is not a ladder culminating in humans.
7. **The first breath.** Placental exchange gives way to lungs and altered circulation at birth. Potentially powerful, but a developmental story with substantial factual demands rather than generic organ assembly.
8. **Changing explanations.** Follow historical investigators from apparently independent parts to circulation and internal regulation. Do not turn incorrect historical models into factual recipes.

## Precedents to investigate

- Silverthorn, *Human Physiology: An Integrated Approach*: common principles, then systems, then explicit integration.
- Sherwood, *Human Physiology: From Cells to Systems*: recurrent homeostatic purpose and each system's contribution to the whole.
- Zahourek, *Anatomy in Clay*: constructive anatomy combining muscles, vessels and nerves in their spatial relationships.
- OpenStax anatomy/physiology: an accessible factual reference, subject to checking the licence of the particular edition used.
- Spongelab *Build-a-Body*: related assembly premise. Availability and actual interaction remain to be checked; do not claim novelty or personal play experience from a description.

## Lessons carried into this study

- The project's [philosophy](../../README.md#philosophy) asks for understanding worth carrying beyond play. The [authoring guide](../../docs/story-authoring.md) supplies the adopted practice.
- A valuable finished object does not make every digital fitting valuable. The Contraptions failure is a reason to test a complete small arc before expanding anatomy coverage.
- Recognizable subjects, meaningful material relationships and consequential discoveries have stronger precedent than invented tokens whose significance exists only in explanatory prose.
- High AI scores do not overrule a weak human premise. Chapter playtests cannot establish whole-story attachment or human enjoyment.
- No graphics in this phase. If a step depends on a picture of a precise spatial fitting, it cannot earn a text-play success by asking the reader to imagine that picture.
