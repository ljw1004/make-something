# Undissection — active design brief

## Intent and audience

Build a recognizable body from connected parts and discover what makes an ordinary action possible. The current prototype, **Lift a glass**, targets grades **8–10** with basic school biology. Its useful ideas are opposing muscular pulls, information passing through nerves, and food and oxygen reaching the same working muscles through blood.

The user rejected the earlier kidney episode as far too advanced. This is a new organ/system-level design, not a vocabulary edit. The [school scope](sources/school-scope.md) supplies the curriculum boundary and factual cautions. The old research remains author background, not the player's syllabus. Earlier reports in the working notes describe a different audience and manuscript and are not evidence for this version.

## Playable arc

1. Bones and a joint become an arm. Muscles on opposite sides allow active bending and straightening; fitting either muscle first must work.
2. Brain, nerves and eyes connect information to the arm. This establishes direction and coordination before the final act, not a late brain-as-life-switch.
3. Heart and blood vessels make a circuit; lungs and breathing muscles provide air exchange. Joining them gives working tissues an oxygen-delivery route and carbon-dioxide return.
4. Stomach and intestine prepare and absorb a meal over time. Their supplies join the same blood route, which connects to the earlier arm and its control. The assembled body finally reaches for and lifts a glass, then reveals that holding it still also requires muscle pulls and energy.

The concrete hook is making an ordinary movement understandable: the hand is where the result is seen, but the work involves structures throughout the body. Keep the early opposed-pull discovery and the final convergence. Do not add other systems merely to complete a catalogue. Kidney microanatomy, named hormone pathways, molecular transport, ATP bookkeeping and chemical reaction sequences are outside scope.

## Abstraction and voice

- This is a working explanatory model of selected systems in one body. Assembly order is a way to reveal relationships, not surgery, development or resurrection. Mechanical possibilities shown early do not claim that disconnected organs live or work indefinitely.
- Tendons, supporting tissues, ordinary cellular function, other digestive glands and unexamined muscles may be included in supplied anatomical assemblies. State the inclusion where relevant to prevent a misleading recipe, without turning each explanation into a disclaimer. The arm supplies the representative skeletal/muscular part of the body; this is not exhaustive anatomy.
- Muscles pull through tendons. Opposing muscles demonstrate active bend/straighten, not a universal rule that one muscle fully switches off or that lowering a weight always uses the opposite muscle. Brain/nerves coordinate; the heart has its own rhythm.
- Blood carries oxygen and nutrients to tissues and carries carbon dioxide back toward the lungs. It is a returning circuit. Digestion and absorption take time; existing stores can fuel movement before a new meal is absorbed.
- A result should normally explain one relationship in everyday language. Define a necessary new term at first player-visible use; optional encyclopedia text must not contain a missing prerequisite for the next move. Avoid specialist labels even when the surrounding sentence is easy.
- Do not promise learning effectiveness or age suitability from an adult AI's ability to complete recipes. Review prerequisites explicitly and retain the need for target-age human evidence.
